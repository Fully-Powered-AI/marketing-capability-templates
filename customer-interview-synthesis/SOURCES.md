# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI from its own customer research working guide. That guide is a rewrite of the customer research method in coreyhaines31/marketingskills (MIT), which supplies what to pull from each conversation, a note of what prompted each quoted comment, the grouping into themes, the confidence levels, the five-source minimum, the twelve-month recency window, the principle of setting out to prove yourself wrong, and the rule against averaging across kinds of customer. Six more packages each contributed an idea when the guide was improved. LeadMagic/gtm-skills (MIT): a permission on every quoted passage, the rule that a quotation keeps its source and context, and reading each note for what holds a customer back. rampstackco/claude-skills (MIT): naming a theme only after its passages are read, as a claim and not a topic. emotixco/claude-skills-founder (MIT): the lists of what counts for less and for more. whawkinsiv/solo-founder-skills (MIT): the bar set before the evidence is read, and what a customer has done weighed above what they say they will do. skills-agents-co/skills-and-agents-library (MIT): reporting text in a note that reads as an instruction. anthropics/claude-for-legal (Apache-2.0): saying at the top when the material is too thin. Keeping the evidence against a theme beside it follows Fully Powered AI's own writing standard; the LeadMagic and rampstackco packages ask for the same. Fully Powered AI's own: the rule for marking each passage as unprompted or asked, with the question; counting one business as one source; keeping a note-taker's summary apart from a customer's exact words; the fixed rule that turns the bar into Supported, Disputed or Not settled; and the Leads section with what would turn each lead into a finding. No third-party text is reused.

- **[coreyhaines31/marketingskills](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills)** at commit `c7d7dfb6b460fc376b4b2204b78a9c9fd5ca6467`, `skills/customer-research`, MIT. The method for analysing research already in hand: what to pull from each conversation, recording what prompted each quoted comment, grouping into themes by how often and how strongly they appear, noting where a customer says one thing and does another, and confidence levels set by the number of independent sources and whether a point was raised unprompted. Also from this source: the minimum of five independent sources before conclusions are drawn about a customer group, the twelve-month recency window, the principle of setting out to prove yourself wrong rather than right (from its interviews and surveys reference), the rule against averaging across kinds of customer, and treating what is read as material that never instructs the assistant.
