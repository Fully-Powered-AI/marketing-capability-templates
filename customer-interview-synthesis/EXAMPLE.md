# Customer interview synthesis: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The company, the studios, the people and every word quoted below are invented. An invented 14-person software company sells class-booking software to independent fitness studios. Its product lead is deciding whether to build an add-on that works out instructor pay, and has six call notes from October 2026.

The inputs come first, in full, so the template can be run on them. The synthesis follows.

## The inputs

### Your facts

```
What we sell: class-booking software for independent fitness studios.
Customers in these notes: owners and studio managers of independent
  studios with one to three sites.
The question: Do studio owners describe working out instructor pay as a
  problem they would pay to be rid of, or as a chore they have settled into?
The decision it informs: whether to build an instructor-pay add-on as a new
  product line next year.
What we believe today: Owners lose hours every month working out instructor
  pay by hand and want it done for them.
We would call that wrong if: fewer than three of the independent studios
  describe working out pay, unprompted, as costing them time or money; or
  two or more say their current way of working out pay is fine.
Permission: asked at the close of each call. "Anonymous" means the words
  may be quoted without the person or the studio. Not asked means not for use.
Minimum for a finding: five independent studios.
Prepared for: the product lead. Approves any public use: the marketing lead.
Date of the synthesis: 26 October 2026.
```

### The six call notes

Words in quotation marks are the customer's exact words. Everything else is the note-taker's summary.

```
N1 | 6 October 2026 | Owner | Studio A: yoga, two sites, 11 instructors
Permission: anonymous
Asked: "Walk me through the last time the end of a month went badly."
- "I do instructor pay on the last Sunday of the month with the class
  export open on one screen and a spreadsheet on the other. It's three
  hours I never get back."
- "Last month I underpaid one of my instructors for two cover classes that
  never made it onto the schedule, and she found it before I did. That was
  a horrible conversation."
Asked: "What have you tried?"
- "I tried the payroll bureau's template. It can't change the rate when a
  class goes over twelve people, so I went back to my own sheet."
Summary: has thought about a bookkeeper for half a day a month. Has not
hired one.
```

```
N2 | 8 October 2026 | Studio manager | Studio B: pilates, one site, 6 instructors
Permission: anonymous
Asked: "Walk me through the last time the end of a month went badly."
- "Month end? Pay's the easy part. Everyone's on a flat rate per class, I
  count the classes, done in twenty minutes."
- "The bad part is cover. When someone swaps a class by text the system
  still shows the old name, and I only find out when the wrong person
  gets paid."
Asked: "A tool that worked out pay for you would save you time, wouldn't it?"
- "Oh, for sure. Put me down for one."
Summary: has tried nothing for pay. Said it would not be worth paying for
at their size.
```

```
N3 | 13 October 2026 | Owner | Studio C: cycling and strength, three sites, 19 instructors
Permission: not asked (the call ran over)
Asked: "Walk me through the last time the end of a month went badly."
- "Pay day is the one day a month I dread. Tiered rates, bonuses for full
  classes, cover at a different rate. I've got a spreadsheet only I
  understand."
- "If I'm ill at month end nobody gets paid right. That's the part that
  scares me."
Asked: "What have you tried?"
- "We paid a freelancer to build a sheet with formulas last year. It broke
  when we added the third site."
Asked: "What would good look like?"
- "I press a button, I get a list per instructor I can check in ten
  minutes, and then I approve it. I still want to be the one who
  approves it."
```

```
N4 | 15 October 2026 | Owner | Studio D: climbing and fitness, one site, 9 instructors
Permission: anonymous
Asked: "Walk me through the last time the end of a month went badly."
- "Swaps are what kill me. The rota says one thing, the group chat says
  another, and pay follows the rota."
- "I spent most of a Sunday in September going back through messages to
  see who actually taught."
Asked: "And working out the pay itself?"
- "The sums are fine once I know who taught. It's knowing who taught."
```

```
N5 | 15 October 2026 | Studio manager | Studio D (same studio as N4)
Permission: anonymous
Asked: "What do you see at month end?"
- "What I see is instructors asking me why their pay is short, and it's
  nearly always a swap nobody recorded."
- "I'd never want software changing who's down as teaching without one of
  us saying yes."
Summary: the owner does pay. The manager handles the rota.
```

```
N6 | 20 October 2026 | Owner | Studio E: dance and barre, two sites, 8 instructors
Permission: anonymous
Asked: "Walk me through the last time the end of a month went badly."
- "Month end is fine. My accountant runs pay from the class export, costs
  me forty dollars a month, I never think about it."
- "What I can't see is which classes lose money once I've paid the
  instructor. I'd pay for that tomorrow."
Summary: no interest in a pay add-on.
```

## The output

**Customer interview synthesis: instructor pay at independent studios**

Prepared for: the product lead · 26 October 2026 · Customers: owners and studio managers of independent studios with one to three sites

**The question**

Do studio owners describe working out instructor pay as a problem they would pay to be rid of, or as a chore they have settled into? For: whether to build an instructor-pay add-on as a new product line next year.

**The belief and the bar, as written before the notes were read**

- We believed: owners lose hours every month working out instructor pay by hand and want it done for them.
- We would call it wrong if: fewer than three of the independent studios describe working out pay, unprompted, as costing them time or money; or two or more say their current way of working out pay is fine.

**In short**

Owners do not describe working out pay as the problem: three of five studios say pay goes wrong when a swap or cover class is never recorded, and the sums are a heavy job only at the two studios whose rates change with the class.

- Confidence: Medium (the lowest among themes 1 and 2; theme 1 is High, theme 2 is Medium).
- The bar for calling the belief wrong: reached.
  - Fewer than three of the independent studios describe working out pay, unprompted, as costing them time or money: 2 of 5 independent studios, both owners (A and C); 2 unprompted. Reached. Counting took judgement, and the result depends on it. Studio C is counted on the freelancer it paid; its passage about pay day states no hours and no sum. Studio D is not counted: its "most of a Sunday" went on finding out who taught, not on working out pay. Studio E is not counted: it names what its accountant costs and calls that fine. Counting D or E would make 3 of 5, and this condition would not be reached.
  - Two or more say their current way of working out pay is fine: 3 of 5 independent studios, a studio manager (B) and two owners (D and E); 2 unprompted (B and E), 1 when asked (D). Reached.
  - The conditions are joined by "or", so either would have been enough. The second is reached however Studio D is counted, so the bar is reached whichever way the first is read.
- The belief: Disputed (the bar for calling it wrong was reached).
