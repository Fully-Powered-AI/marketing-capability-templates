# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words, from the creator-vetting method in its own influencer-marketing set, which it publishes under the MIT licence. That method drew on four published repositories, credited here for their ideas. ScrapeCreators/social-media-research-skills (MIT): scoring every creator on a fixed set of lines, among them audience fit, engagement quality, brand safety and contactability, with the cautions that size is not fit and that only public contact routes count (influencer-prospecting); and judging an audience from what can actually be seen, so that how sure the judgment is depends on what the platform shows (audience-research); and treating an unusually strong post as an outlier whose cause is not assumed, which supports taking the median of per-post rates (creator-profile-teardown). unifapi-agent/agents (MIT), its port of the KOL Pricing framework: a fixed rule for tiers, an engagement rate worked out from posts, a confidence level that falls when the evidence is thin, and the engage, negotiate and skip split (kol-pricing). aaron-he-zhu/aaron-marketing-skills (Apache-2.0): when evidence cannot be reached, the item is left open rather than failed or given a cautious middle score; every item carries a dated source; and a creator's suitability is kept apart from the terms of any deal (fit-scorer). coreyhaines31/marketingskills (MIT): the vetting signals, namely the rate weighed above the size of the following, counts that look rounded off, repeated or mismatched comments, unexplained follower jumps, media kits that flatter, sponsored posts the audience passes over, three months of posts read for brand safety, competitor conflicts, and creators' own text read as something to assess, not to obey (influencer-marketing). Fully Powered AI added: the six lines as used here, with authenticity and sponsored track record, and fixed anchors for each; the median of per-post rates, with views as the divisor on video platforms; the three confidence checks; the exclude verdict, the authenticity floor and the rules that hold an engage at negotiate; the brand-safety flag and the questions sent to the brand's owner; the checks where a score and the numbers disagree; a label on every number; and the rule that a person confirms every score and chooses who is approached. No third-party text is reused.
