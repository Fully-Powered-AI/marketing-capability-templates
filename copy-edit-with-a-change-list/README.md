# Copy edit with a change list

Hand over a page, an email or a brochure and get it back plainer and tighter, with no claim stronger than the statements you can support and a change list that shows every edit, its reason and any meaning it moved. You approve each change before the copy is used.

*Capability template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/copy-edit-with-a-change-list/)*

## The outcome

The person who signs off copy stops guessing what an editor changed. The draft comes back clearer, in one voice, with the filler gone and the vague words replaced by facts from your own records. Beside it sits a change list: the line before, the line after, why it changed and which rule applied.

Claims are checked before anything is polished. Each one is held to a short list of statements your company can stand behind, and an edit never makes a claim bigger, wider or more certain than that list allows. When a line promises more than you can show, the change list says so, proposes the fix and names what would let the original stand. Every edit that dropped or added a fact sits in its own list, so nothing shifts in meaning without someone seeing it. You approve, change or reject each edit, and nothing is published until you do.

## Who starts the work, and when

Whoever drafted or owns the copy starts it: a marketing coordinator, a marketing lead, or an owner who writes their own pages. It runs before a page goes live, before an email or brochure goes out, when existing copy needs a refresh because the business changed, or when a draft reads as if a machine wrote it. The person who approves copy reads the result and decides.

The capability edits a copy of your text. It publishes nothing, sends nothing and changes no page.

## What they supply

- **The copy,** in full and in order, with its headings and buttons.
- **Where it appears, who reads it and the one thing they should do next.**
- **Your facts.** What you sell, who it is for, your voice in a few words and your house style.
- **Statements you can support.** One line for each thing you can stand behind, what backs it up and when it was last checked, and the things you never say, such as guarantee wording.
- **Approved wording.** Your tagline, offer names, prices as published and any line someone has already signed off, which stay word for word.

## What comes out

One document in a fixed order:

1.  **A verdict:** ready to approve, ready with gaps, or not ready, with a count of the lines changed and the claims checked.
2.  **The edited copy,** in its original order, with a marked gap wherever a fact is needed and you have not yet supplied it.
3.  **The change list,** one entry per changed line, each with its reason and rule.
4.  **Every claim with its verdict:** kept, brought back to what you can support, or proposed for cutting, with the statement that decided it.
5.  **What moved in meaning, the claims that need support, the gaps,** and the habits that made the draft read as machine-written, each with its fix.
6.  **The decisions left to you,** and who makes each.

Good looks like this: the approver can read the change list alone, accept or reject each line, and know that nothing in the copy claims more than the company can show.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your statements list.** Write the things you can stand behind, one per line, with what supports each and when it was checked. The edit is only as good as this list.
- **Your "do not say" list.** Guarantee wording, superlatives, savings figures, or anything your sector restricts.
- **Your approved wording.** The lines nobody edits without the owner's say.
- **Who approves.** The role that accepts or rejects each change and settles each gap.
- **Your house style.** Spelling, dashes, how figures are written, and the words you use for your customers.
- **Your platform.** It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Decide which drafts and statements the assistant may read and where approved copy is kept.
