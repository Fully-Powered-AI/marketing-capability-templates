# Creator publication tracking

A brand marketer sees, creator by creator, what was agreed against what actually went live: each post checked for its disclosure, code, link and approved version, dated checks that it is still up, and every gap set out as a fix to ask for or a decision to make. A person sends every message.

*Capability template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/creator-publication-tracking/)*

## The outcome

Approval covers the draft; this covers what the public actually sees. For every post a creator agreed to publish, the report says whether it went up, when, and whether it carries what was agreed: the disclosure in every form your rules require, the discount code and tracked link exactly as issued, and the version you approved. A post that is right on every line simply drops off the to-do list.

Every gap comes with its next step. A missing label or a wrong code becomes a fix request, drafted for the programme lead to send, with a date to fix it by. A post that says something its approved version did not becomes a note for the content reviewer. A post that disappears becomes a referral with the facts and your own term quoted, never a verdict on the creator. The follow-up checks on day 1, 7 and 30 sit on the same page, so nothing quietly lapses.

The record holds up. A fix counts only when someone has looked at the post again, never on the creator's word. A line nobody looked at is shown as not looked at, never filled in from the brief. At the end, each creator is marked complete or not, so the person who decides fees has the facts in one place.

## Who starts the work, and when

The creator programme lead, or the brand marketer who runs creators, starts it when a post goes live, on the days its checks fall due, when a creator says a fix is done, when a post vanishes, and any time they want the state of a campaign. They keep a simple post log as they look at each post, read the report, and send whatever needs sending from the brand's own account.

## What they supply

- **Your campaign rules:** the disclosure forms required for each platform and format, the accepted wording, the words that do not count, how many days a creator has to fix a live post, the minimum days a post stays up, your check days, and who reviews content and who decides fees.
- **The deliverables list:** each agreed post with its creator, platform, format, date, issued code and link, and the approved version.
- **The post log:** one row each time someone looked at a live post, recording what they saw on each line.
- **The actions log,** if there is one: fixes already asked for, referrals made and any items the owner waived.
- **The report date.**

## What comes out

One report in a fixed order: agreed against published, the live check for each post as last seen, the dated checks, then everything sorted into overdue, act now, not checked and coming up. Below that sit removed or edited posts, the drafted fix requests, notes and referrals, delivery by creator, and a coverage list of what was used, what was missing and what could not be judged. Nothing is sent, posted, paid or changed.

## What has to change for your company

Write down your disclosure forms for each platform and format, with your wording and the words that do not count; the capability adds none of its own. Disclosure rules differ from country to country, so a person at your company decides whether your rules suit each country your creators reach. Set your correction window and the minimum days a post stays up, and quote the matching terms you sign with creators. Choose your check days and decide whether stories, which can vanish by themselves, get the later checks. Agree who keeps the post log, who sends fix requests and who decides fees. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the files you supply, with no access to your social accounts or your store.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
