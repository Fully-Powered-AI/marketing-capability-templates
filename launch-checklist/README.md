# Launch checklist

Whoever owns a launch date gets one dated checklist for the weeks before, the day itself and the weeks after, with an owner on every line. Each announcement is written out so the approver sees exactly what goes out, to whom and from where before saying go. Nothing is announced by the assistant.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/launch-checklist/)*

## The outcome

A launch stops being a day you hope goes well and becomes a sequence your team can see. Every step has a date counted from the launch day, skips the days you are closed, and names the person who does it. The page starts with a plain verdict on whether the thing is ready to show, and if one small cut would make it ready, it names the cut.

Launches here are sized to what they deserve. A new service gets the full sequence; a small improvement gets a quiet line on your list of changes. Channels are chosen by who controls them, so every post or partner mention leads people back to your own page, where the one action you want sits waiting.

Every line that reaches someone outside the business is spelled out: the draft, the group and how many people are in it, the address it goes from, and what happens when they reply. Your approver knows exactly what they are saying yes to. The assistant sends, posts, publishes and schedules nothing.

## Who starts the work, and when

The person who owns the launch date starts it: an owner, a marketing lead or a project lead. Large launches start about three weeks ahead, medium ones about ten days ahead. It runs again whenever the date, the scope or the audience moves, and the new version lists what changed.

The launch owner settles every question and approves the checklist before it goes to the team.

## What they supply

- **The launch brief.** What you are launching, who it is for, the one action you want them to take, the launch day, who approves anything that goes out, and the days you are closed.
- **Three quick answers on size.** How much of your audience it touches, whether it is new, and whether it changes what you sell or how people buy.
- **Your facts.** A short note in your own words: what you sell, who buys, the proof you may show and the terms the launch must match.
- **The team.** Each role and what it does, so every line gets an owner.
- **Your audience list.** Each group you can reach, how you know them, how many there are and how you reach them, plus any channel you will not use.
- **Where the launch stands,** the dates any partner or newsletter needs, earlier launches, and the pages and sales documents the launch might contradict.

## What comes out

One checklist in a fixed order:

1. **The launch in one line, its size and its readiness.** The verdict comes first, with each reason tied to something you supplied.
2. **The channels,** each marked owned, borrowed or rented, with the groups and counts from your list and the reason any channel was left out.
3. **Before, the day and after.** Every line dated and owned; every outward line spelled out and waiting for the approver.
4. **Drafts needed,** with who writes each and by when.
5. **What the launch makes wrong,** such as an old price on a live page, each picked up by a line before the day.
6. **Questions for the launch owner,** each with the facts behind it.

A good checklist can be read by every owner in two minutes, and nobody is surprised by their name or their date.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your lead times and review date.** The defaults are three weeks before a large launch and ten days before a medium one, with the review two or four weeks after. Set your own.
- **Your standard lines.** Add the steps your business always takes, such as a sales briefing or a stock check.
- **Your audience list.** Keep it current, by group and count, never person by person.
- **Who approves.** Name the person who says go on anything that reaches someone outside the business.
- **Where replies are recorded,** so the review can count what each channel brought back.
- **Your platform.** It is written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, working from the notes and lists you supply. It needs no access to your website or email tools.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
