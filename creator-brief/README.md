# Creator brief

A brand marketer gets one brief per creator for a campaign: the product statements the brand has cleared, word for word, the disclosure that creator's platform needs, their own code and link, the dates and the review round. Nothing reaches a creator until a person reads it and sends it.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/creator-brief/)*

## The outcome

Every creator in a campaign starts from the same clear page, made for them. It tells them what the campaign is for, what to deliver and by when, the few things about the product they may say in exact words, what is off limits, and how to label the post as a partnership on their platform. Everything else is theirs: the idea, the voice, the look. The brief asks for their own experience of the product and never hands them a script.

The statements in the brief are only ones a named person at the brand has cleared, for the exact product shown. A point the campaign wanted but nobody has cleared stays out and arrives on the marketer's desk as a question for whoever clears statements. Each creator sees only their own code and link. Pay and usage stay in the signed agreement; the brief names it by date and adds nothing to it.

The brief also checks itself against your rules before you read it, and its first line says whether it is ready for your read or what is still missing.

## Who starts the work, and when

The brand marketer or the creator programme lead starts it once creators have agreed to a campaign and the brand has written down its cleared statements and its disclosure wording for each platform. It runs again when the plan, a statement or a disclosure rule changes before posts go live. The programme lead reads every brief, removes the notes and sends it from the brand's own account.

## What they supply

- **Your creator rules:** the cleared statements, each marked with who cleared it and when; the statements that are forbidden, with reasons; your ruling on each platform; your disclosure wording, placement and spoken form for each platform; your redlines and brand voice; and who rules on platforms, who issues creator codes and links, and who keeps signed agreements.
- **The campaign plan:** why the campaign exists in the owner's words, the product shown, the deliverables and dates, the call to action, the code discount and the points you want made.
- **Your creator roster export,** with each creator's platforms, status, code, link and the date their agreement was signed.
- **The request:** which creators, which platform each brief is for, the brief date and who will read and send the briefs.

## What comes out

A run summary, then one brief per creator in a fixed order: what the campaign is for, room to make it their own, deliverables and dates, the product statements they may use, what is off limits, their code and link, the disclosure, your platform and audience rules, the review before posting, a line naming their agreement, and who to ask. Each brief opens with its status and ends with notes for the sender: the sources used, a twelve-point self-check, any gaps, and statements waiting to be cleared.

A creator on a platform you have not ruled on, or with no disclosure wording for their platform, gets no brief, and the run summary says why. A gap is marked as missing, never filled with a guess.

## What has to change for your company

Write down your cleared statements and name the person who clears them; the workflow template adds none of its own. Set your disclosure wording and placement for each platform you use. Disclosure rules differ from country to country, so a person at your company decides whether your wording fits each country your creators reach, taking advice where needed. Decide how many fixed statements a brief may carry; fewer leave more room for the creator. Match the roster columns to your own export. Name who reads and sends briefs. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from files you supply, with no access to your social accounts or your store.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
