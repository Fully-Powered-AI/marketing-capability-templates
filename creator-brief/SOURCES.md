# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words, from the creator brief in its own Influencer Marketing set, which it publishes under the MIT licence as the influencer-marketing repository. That brief took ideas from four published packages, credited here. From aaron-he-zhu/aaron-marketing-skills (Apache-2.0): the parts of a creator brief (key messages, what not to say, deliverables, creative freedom, timeline, the review round, disclosure and contact) and the principle that a brief guides the creator rather than scripting them. From coreyhaines31/marketingskills (MIT): the purpose in one sentence, a small ceiling on talking points, a call to action carrying the creator's own code and link, guardrails, creative freedom granted in so many words, and where a disclosure has to sit to count, with the tags that do not count on their own. From Orkas-AI/Orkas (MIT): the point that a picture or demonstration can make a claim the words may not, and that every claim belongs to the exact product shown. From finsilabs/awesome-ecommerce-skills (MIT): the checks made on creator content before it is used (no competitor products, nothing inappropriate, no misleading claims), which the brief's lines on competitors and redlines rest on. Fully Powered AI added the rule that only statements a named person has cleared appear, word for word and per product; disclosure taken from the brand's own rules for each platform, with the question of each country's rules left to a person; one brief per creator, so nobody sees another creator's code; the twelve-point self-check and status line; the stops for an unruled platform or missing disclosure; and the rule that the brief names the signed agreement by date and states no terms. No third-party text is reused.
