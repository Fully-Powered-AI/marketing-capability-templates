# Content plan

A marketing lead with a few hours a week gets a content plan built on the questions buyers really ask: one subject for each question worth answering, the formats those hours can carry, a ranked list of what to write next, and what is held or left out, each with its reason.

*Capability template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/content-plan/)*

## The outcome

You stop writing whatever comes to mind on a free afternoon. The plan starts from two facts: the hours you actually have, and the questions buyers keep asking your sales and support people. Every question appears in the buyer's own words, with where and when it was heard, and every subject in the plan answers one of them.

The plan uses the hours you give it and sets no figure of its own. It adds up what each piece costs you and stops when the period's hours run out, so the list under "write next" is one you can finish. Pieces that wait on something, such as a client's permission or a process nobody has written down, are held with the exact thing they wait on. Ideas that no buyer asked for are set aside, each with its reason.

The plan proposes; you decide. It writes no piece, schedules nothing and publishes nothing.

## Who starts the work, and when

The marketing lead, or the owner who does the company's own marketing, starts it at the start of a quarter, when a new offer changes what buyers ask, or when a pile of content ideas needs sorting. Whoever hears buyers' questions, in sales calls, support emails or the website chat, supplies them. The marketing lead approves the plan, and each piece is approved again before it goes public.

## What they supply

- **The hours.** How much time you can give each week, the form you make most easily, any form you will not make, and how long one piece of each format takes you.
- **Your facts.** A few lines: what you sell and to whom, who finds you first and who decides, what you can show, what you must never say, the one place you own where pieces live, and what a reader should do next.
- **The buyer questions.** Call notes, support emails, chat logs or a question log exported from a spreadsheet.
- **What already exists.** The pieces you have published and the material on hand: examples, checklists, recordings, answers already written.
- **Ideas already proposed,** with who proposed each, if there are any.

## What comes out

One plan for the period, in a fixed order:

1. **In short,** with the hours planned out of the hours you have.
2. **The buyer questions,** grouped, counted, placed at the moment in the buying process where they come up, and checked against what you have already published.
3. **The subjects,** each with a promise, what it adds to what already exists, the material it draws on, a limit on what it must never say, and whether it is meant to be found or passed along to the person who decides.
4. **The formats and the one place** your pieces live, with other channels only pointing readers there.
5. **The backlog:** write next in order with hours and a done-by week, next period, and held pieces with what each waits on.
6. **Not written,** each with its reason; one question to ask on every first call as the measure; a coverage line; and the decisions left to you.

Good looks like this: you can read any line under "write next" and see the buyer question behind it, the hours it takes and the week it should be done.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your hours, honestly.** The plan is only as accurate as the hours you give it and the time each format really takes you.
- **Where questions are kept.** A shared log with a date, a source, the buyer's role and the question in their words. Any columns work if you say which is which.
- **Your "never say" list.** Results you cannot show, client names without permission, advice you do not give in public.
- **The one place you own,** and which other channels only point to it.
- **Who approves.** The person who approves the plan, and the person who approves each piece before it goes public.
- **Your platform.** It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from the notes and files you paste or attach; it needs no connection to your website or social accounts.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
