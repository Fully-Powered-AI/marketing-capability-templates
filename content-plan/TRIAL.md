# Content plan: one run on the invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Template version:** 0.1.0

This is a record of one run of the capability template on its own invented example. The inputs are the invented example's; the firm, its people and every question are invented, and no client data was used. Nothing was written, scheduled, published or sent, and no one was contacted.

An earlier run the same day, on the template before an editorial review's points were applied, found several places where the instructions had to be guessed at. The template was changed, and the run recorded here was made on the changed text. Both are described below: first this run, then the earlier run's findings and the change each led to.

**A limit on this record.** The earlier run was made by a model that did not write the template, and it read the example's output only after producing its own. This run was not blind: it was made in the same session that applied the review's points, after reading both the example's output and the earlier run's record. It shows what the revised instructions give when followed step by step. It is not an independent second reading of them.

## Inputs used

The inputs in [the example](example.md), under "The inputs": the hours, "your facts", the settings, the eleven-line question log, what already exists, and the two proposed ideas. The date in the output is the date of the run, not the example's.

## What was observed

Every part of the output format was produced, in order, with no stop condition reached. The question log's headers matched the defaults, the hours and the hours per piece were given, and nothing in the inputs read as an instruction. The sums and the done-by weeks were checked by hand after the run: 9, 12 and 15 hours, divided by two hours a week and rounded up, give weeks 5, 6 and 8 of a period whose first week starts on Monday 16 November 2026.

Where this run differed from the published example:

- **Format for the switching piece, and the hours.** The example plans S3 as an article with posts (9 hours) and uses 21 of the 26 hours. This run planned it as a checklist (3 hours), because the material on hand is already a list of steps, and used 15 hours. The template lets either format be chosen when the person makes both easily and both hours are known; it gives no rule for choosing between them. This choice, not the grouping, is now what separates the two totals.
- **Found or passed on for the client story.** The example has S5 passed on from the general manager to the owner. This run marked it found, because the owner asked the question directly.
- **Limits.** The wording of several limits differs; each comes from "your facts" or from the bounds of the material on hand, as the revised step allows.
- **Next step for the reader.** The example has the general manager pass the point-of-sale checklist to the owner, who then books a call. This run used the single next step in "your facts" for all three pieces.
- **Not written and holds.** The same: the taxes question and the trends idea under Not written, the tips piece and the client story held. The tips hold now names a described role marked as not named in "your facts", as the example does.
- **Date and week dates.** The example is dated 6 November; this run used 9 November. The third done-by week differs only because the third piece's hours differ.

Facts in this output that are not in the inputs:

- That nothing published answers the point-of-sale, switching or tips questions. The inputs describe "Our fees" only as the fee and a list of what is included, so this was read from that description.

Ambiguities and judgments, as met:

- **The grouping test still takes judgment in one place.** "What does a monthly report from you look like?" was grouped under Q1 because a piece written to answer what an owner gets each month would show the monthly pack, which answers what the report looks like. Read from the other side (a piece written only about the report), the match is weaker. The grouping gives Q1 its high rating and puts S1 first.
- **Choosing between two formats that both fit** has no rule (see above), and it moves the hours planned.
- **The one-sentence test for "places like ours".** One sentence on "Our fees" could say whom the firm serves, but the buyer asked whether it had worked with places like theirs, which a sentence states and a client story shows. This run made it a subject, merged with the sales lead's idea; the earlier run put it under Not written. Both readings fit the steps.
- **Material the company holds but did not supply,** such as how long a switch usually takes, is still not covered by the ratings. Here the limit kept the switching piece to the steps.

## The earlier run, and what was changed after it

The first run that day was made by Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5), also in Claude Code, on the template before the review's points were applied. It produced every part of the format. It kept "What does a monthly report from you look like?" apart from the question about what an owner gets each month, which gave seven groups instead of six, a medium rating instead of high for the most-asked question, a different order, and 15 of 26 hours planned. It recorded these places where it had to guess, and each led to a change:

- "Group questions that ask the same thing" had no test. The template now says two questions ask the same thing when a piece written to answer one would also answer the other, and the reviewer checklist asks for each group to be checked against that test.
- No rule placed a single question at a buyer moment. The template now says what each moment covers and that a group of one takes its question's moment.
- The example's limit on the switching piece did not come from "your facts". The template now lets a limit come from "your facts" or from the bounds of the material on hand, and the example says which bound each such limit rests on.
- "Posts cut from it where the person makes posts" did not say what to do when hours are given only for posts cut from an article. Posts are now added only to formats with an hours figure for them.
- A hold had to name a payroll role that "your facts" do not give. The template now says to describe the role and mark it "role not named", with the approver deciding who.
- It was unclear whether "fits in one sentence on an existing page" covers a sentence the page does not yet have. It now does, and adding the sentence goes to the decisions.
- "Done by: week n, starting [date]" read as a deadline. The format now reads "Done by: in week n, which starts [date]", and the steps say the date marks the week's first day, not a deadline.

The reviewer checklist was also corrected so that only write-next, next-period and held candidates must carry four ratings, as the Not written line carries none. Two of the earlier run's other notes are not changed: the format choice between an article and a checklist, and material the company holds but did not supply.

This was one run by one model on an invented example. It does not show how the template behaves with a real client's material or in another product.
