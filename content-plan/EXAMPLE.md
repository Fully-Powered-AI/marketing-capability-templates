# Content plan: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The firm, its clients, its people and every question below are invented. An invented 12-person firm keeps the books and runs payroll for independent restaurants. Its marketing lead works on content two hours a week and wants a plan for the next 13 weeks.

The inputs come first, in full, so the template can be run on them. The plan follows.

## The inputs

### The hours

```
"I can give two hours a week to content, Tuesday mornings. An article
takes me about eight hours start to finish, a checklist about three, and
cutting an article into a few LinkedIn posts about one. I write. I won't
do video."
(the marketing lead, 6 November 2026)
```

### Your facts

```
What we sell: monthly bookkeeping and payroll for independent restaurants
  with one to four locations, for a fixed monthly fee.
Who finds us first and who decides: the general manager often finds us;
  the owner decides.
What we can show: a sample month-end pack built with invented figures for
  demonstrations; our onboarding checklist for new clients.
What we must never say: tax advice in public pieces (we refer clients to
  their tax preparer); any client's name or results without their written
  permission; that we save a restaurant a set amount; comparisons naming
  another firm.
The one place we own: the Guides page on our website. Live.
Other channels: our company LinkedIn page, posted on now and then. No
  email list yet.
What a reader should do next: book a 20-minute call from the website.
Where leads are recorded: the sales lead's call notes.
Approves the plan: the marketing lead.
Approves each piece before it goes public: the managing partner.
```
