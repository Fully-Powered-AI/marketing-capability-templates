# Customer case study: a trial on the invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

The inputs are the invented example in [example.md](example.md): Firm F, Client K, the people and every word quoted are invented. No client data was used.

This record replaces the record of an earlier run on the same day, made by Claude Sonnet 5.5 on the first version of the template. That run's observations, and a review of the template, led to the revisions below; the template and the example's inputs were then changed, so the run was made again. The run recorded here was made once, by the same assistant that made those revisions in the same session. It had read the earlier run, the earlier example output and the review, so it is not an independent run.

## What was revised before this run

- The opening of the guide now starts with a line the interviewer reads, holding the recording check, and every question and follow-up is open and asks about one thing.
- The short version carries exactly one quotation, and the pasted instructions now say so and say what the short version holds.
- Length is counted from the headline to the last attribution, labels included, leaving out tags and the closing line. The requester's closing line is copied as supplied and takes no tag.
- One result takes one tag for its measure, before, after and periods; a results heading that only repeats the periods takes none; a figure keeps its tag in any wording. Identity items stay on the approval list whether used or not, and the final check covers quotations and numbers only.
- A before period must end before the earliest start of the work; if not, the figures are used as supplied and the overlap is raised for the requester.
- A date is written as the record gives it, with no year or day added.
- A difference between the brief and the record goes under Differences to settle, and the draft follows the record. Rows marked ask or no are named in Not used without their figures.
- In the example's inputs, the figures sheet's before period moved from January to March 2026 (which contained the switch-over) to October to December 2025, and the interview record's question labels were renumbered to match the example's revised guide.

## Inputs used

From `example.md`, only the inputs: the customer's agreement, the brief and settings, the interview record (13 items, 14 October 2026), the figures sheet (6 rows, 20 October 2026) and the identity and settings. The template's instruction block and output format were followed as written.

## What came out

Both parts, complete. The guide has 16 questions over seven parts, adding up to 30 minutes. The draft has a short version of 169 words and a long version of 412 words, counted by a script under the template's rule, against limits of 180 and 350 to 600; three results; 14 quotations; 4 numbers; and 4 identity items. A script checked every quotation in both versions and on the approval list against the interview record: each matches word for word. Nothing in the template's stop conditions was triggered, and no text in the record or the sheet read as an instruction.

## What was observed

The template ran to the end without a stop and produced every part of the output format. The before period (October to December 2025) ends before the earliest start in the brief (February 2026), so there was no overlap to raise.

### Where the instructions were unclear and a guess was needed

- **Periods written in a sentence.** The tagging rule exempts a results heading that repeats the periods, but the long version opens its results paragraph with the periods in a sentence ("Comparing October to December 2025 with July to September 2026"). This run treated them as covered by the result tags that follow and gave them no tag of their own.
- **The customer's words told in the draft's own sentences.** The template allows the interviewer's summary in the draft's own sentences, but does not say whether the customer's exact words may also be retold that way. With one quotation allowed, the short version's situation retells "chasing job sheets instead of chasing money" as "chased job sheets instead of payments", without quotation marks and without any figure.
- **Spoken figures that agree with the sheet.** "The last three days of every month" and "Month end is one day now, not three" agree with row F3. The template says what to do only when a spoken figure disagrees. This run kept both quotations and noted that they agree.
- **A passage that partly agrees.** In record item 8, "about three weeks now" disagrees with 31 days, while "nearer two months" is close to 52 days. This run treated the passage as disagreeing and left it out whole.
- **Question numbers in the record.** The record's labels follow the numbering of the example's own guide (15 questions), not this run's (16), so they could not be used to link answers to this run's questions.
- **The logo.** The agreement speaks of the name only. This run wrote the logo as "not yet asked".
- **Financial details.** The overdraft and payroll quotations are not on the keep-out list. This run used them and listed them for the requester to decide.

### Differences from the published example

- **Guide.** This run has 16 questions and the example 15: this run adds a question asking how the customer would describe the business. The example allots 2, 6, 4, 7, 7, 2 and 2 minutes; this run used 2, 6, 4, 6, 8, 2 and 2. Both start with a line the interviewer reads and keep every question and follow-up to one open thing. This run's closing checks also mention the details that could identify the customer.
- **Short version.** Both have one quotation (the same one), the same three results and the same date without a year. The headline and the situation are worded differently.
- **Long version.** The example cuts the Monday-routine quotation to "reads it, changes what she wants, and sends it"; this run used the whole sentence. The example adds "21 days sooner" as a figure worked out by us (N5); this run added no worked-out figure, so it has four numbers to the example's five.
- **Decisions.** The example asks whether the unsolved point stays and, since the post-run edit listed below, whether the financial details stay; this run asks only whether the financial details stay. Differences to settle and Not used carry the same points in both.

### Facts in either output that are not in the inputs

- In this run: no figure or name that is not in the inputs. Connecting phrases such as "The wait had a cost", "The hardest part came first" and "put that right" are the draft's own wording around the customer's quotations.
- In the example: none found; its connecting phrases ("The wait cost them", "The start was hard") are of the same kind.

### Changed after this run

- The example's guide line on naming and logo was changed from "to decide after the draft" to "naming to decide after the draft; logo not yet asked", to match this run's reading of the agreement.
- Step 10 of the pasted instructions gained a clause the procedure already had: for a row marked ask, say what approving it would change. This run did so for F5 without the clause.
- After an independent review, on the same day, the example's brief gained a first line stating the customer's trade ("Client K, a heating and cooling installer"), which the run's inputs did not have, and the example's guide a matching line under What the brief says. The example's draft gained the note "Part of a longer passage" on Q8, the wording this run used for I2 ("the name is not used"), and a decision on the financial details in Q5, Q7 and Q12, as this run had.
- After the same review, the template's procedure and pasted instructions gained a clause each in steps 8, 10 and 12 settling four of the guesses above, each the way this run guessed it: periods restated in the long version's Results paragraph take no tag of their own; the customer's words may be retold unquoted if the meaning does not change, with any figure in them following the rules for numbers; a spoken figure that agrees with the sheet may be quoted, noted as agreeing; and a quotation with any figure that disagrees with the sheet is left out whole. The pasted instructions also gained four points the procedure already had: what to do with no brief, the default length when none is given, that a recording with no transcript cannot be used, and that a keep-out is listed under Not used without repeating a figure.
- Nothing else in the template or the example was changed after this run, and none of these changes has been run again.

### Limits of this trial

It is one run by one model on one invented example, made by the assistant that revised the template. It shows that the instructions can be followed to a complete output. It does not show that another product or model would behave the same, and it was not tested in a client environment.
