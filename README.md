# Fully Powered Capability Templates: Marketing

A marketing team of three that ships like a team of thirty. These capability templates take the research, strategy and content work behind good marketing and give an AI assistant a precise way to prepare it, with every claim traced to something you can support and a person approving what the market sees.

This repository holds 28 capability templates for marketing teams and the owners who do their own marketing. Each one is a single piece of work with its own folder: what it is for and who starts it, the complete template an AI assistant follows, a worked example with invented data, what to change for your company, what has been checked, and its sources.

## The capabilities

- **[Affiliate onboarding](affiliate-onboarding/)**: A programme owner brings a new affiliate or creator in: the code and link the store gave are checked against the programme rules and a test at checkout, then a welcome pack and a dated first-weeks checklist are prepared. A person sends, creates and pays.
- **[Brand strategy workshop](brand-strategy-workshop/)**: Give your company one answer to why it exists, who it is for and how it sounds. Over about three hours (a planning figure, not yet timed), the owner makes the six choices that define the brand from options an AI assistant drafts, signs off the rest, and leaves with the tests that check it with real buyers.
- **[Competitor profile from public pages](competitor-profile-from-public-pages/)**: Your marketing lead gets a dated profile of a named competitor, built only from its public pages: what they say, sell and charge, kept apart from what that suggests and the questions it raises for you. Any figure no page states is marked unavailable, never estimated.
- **[Content plan](content-plan/)**: A marketing lead with a few hours a week gets a content plan built on the questions buyers really ask: one subject for each question worth answering, the formats those hours can carry, a ranked list of what to write next, and what is held or left out, each with its reason.
- **[Copy edit with a change list](copy-edit-with-a-change-list/)**: Hand over a page, an email or a brochure and get it back plainer and tighter, with no claim stronger than the statements you can support and a change list that shows every edit, its reason and any meaning it moved. You approve each change before the copy is used.
- **[Creator brief](creator-brief/)**: A brand marketer gets one brief per creator for a campaign: the product statements the brand has cleared, word for word, the disclosure that creator's platform needs, their own code and link, the dates and the review round. Nothing reaches a creator until a person reads it and sends it.
- **[Creator campaign performance report](creator-campaign-performance-report/)**: When a creator campaign closes, the marketing lead gets one report built from the store's own orders: what each creator brought in and cost, every creator ranked under a metric set beforehand, and a proposed next step for each. Every number is labelled measured, supplied or estimated.
- **[Creator campaign plan](creator-campaign-plan/)**: Before a creator campaign starts, the marketing lead gets one plan: the goal, each creator's deal and deliverables, a base and a conservative budget checked against the budget supplied, the dates, the set-up before launch and the decisions. A person approves the spend.
- **[Creator content approval](creator-content-approval/)**: A brand marketer gets a written go or no-go on a creator's draft before it goes live: every product statement checked against the brand's cleared list, the disclosure against the brand's own rules, and feedback the creator can act on without losing their voice. A person sends it.
- **[Creator content reuse plan](creator-content-reuse-plan/)**: A brand marketer sees which published creator posts may be reused on which of the brand's channels, read only from the usage rights they supply, with every placement ending before the rights do. Where the rights are silent or unclear, the question goes to whoever reviews contracts.
- **[Creator discovery longlist](creator-discovery-longlist/)**: Before anyone scores or contacts a creator, the brand marketer gets a dated longlist from public pages and the names the team supplied: where each creator posts, how they were found, where their follower count came from, whether they already talk about your category, and why they are on the list.
- **[Creator outreach drafting](creator-outreach-drafting/)**: A brand marketer gets a first message and three follow-ups for each chosen creator, written for the way that creator can be reached, opening on something they made and stating only the brand's standard offer. A person reads every message and sends it from the brand's own account.
- **[Creator programme setup](creator-programme-setup/)**: A brand owner or marketing lead writes the rules for working with creators once, in one file: products and cleared statements, platforms, how creators are paid with the arithmetic behind it, codes, payouts, disclosure, brand safety and roles. Every line is the owner's ruling, marked with its source.
- **[Creator publication tracking](creator-publication-tracking/)**: A brand marketer sees, creator by creator, what was agreed against what actually went live: each post checked for its disclosure, code, link and approved version, dated checks that it is still up, and every gap set out as a fix to ask for or a decision to make. A person sends every message.
- **[Creator roster](creator-roster/)**: The programme owner keeps one record of every creator the brand works with: where each relationship stands, what each creator still owes the brand, what the brand still owes each creator, and when rights and exclusivity end. It is built from the files the owner supplies; nothing is sent or paid.
- **[Creator vetting shortlist](creator-vetting-shortlist/)**: Before anyone contacts a creator, the program lead sees every name on the longlist scored on six lines, each backed by what was seen and when: audience fit, engagement quality, authenticity, brand safety, sponsored track record and contactability. A person chooses who to approach.
- **[Customer case study](customer-case-study/)**: With a customer who has agreed to help, prepare the interview, then turn their words and the figures they supply into a case study in a short and a long version. Every quotation and number is listed for the customer to approve in writing, and nothing is published or named before they do.
- **[Customer interview synthesis](customer-interview-synthesis/)**: Turn a stack of customer call notes into the answer to one question, in your customers’ own words. Every quotation is cited to the note it came from, every theme shows its confidence and the evidence against it, and nothing is claimed that the notes do not say.
- **[Email sequence drafting](email-sequence-drafting/)**: People who asked to hear from you get emails worth opening: a welcome, a nurture sequence or a new customer's onboarding, drafted in your voice, each email with one job and one ask, every claim traced to your own facts, and a clear end. You read, load and send them yourself.
- **[Event plan](event-plan/)**: Whoever owns one event, a talk, a dinner, a webinar or a stand, gets one plan: the goal and target, who should be in the room, every step before, on and after the day with a date and an owner, and how each conversation is followed up. Nothing is booked, paid or sent by the assistant.
- **[Launch checklist](launch-checklist/)**: Whoever owns a launch date gets one dated checklist for the weeks before, the day itself and the weeks after, with an owner on every line. Each announcement is written out so the approver sees exactly what goes out, to whom and from where before saying go. Nothing is announced by the assistant.
- **[Lead magnet plan](lead-magnet-plan/)**: A marketer or owner gets the plan for one free resource built on a question buyers really ask: who it is for, what it promises and delivers, a format the team can make in the hours it has, the outline section by section, and whether it needs a form at all.
- **[Ninety-day marketing plan](ninety-day-marketing-plan/)**: An owner or first marketing hire gets the next ninety days of marketing on one plan: the stage the business is really in, the moves that fit it, who owns each one, the money and hours they use, and what is deliberately left undone. Every count comes from your own records, and no budget is invented.
- **[Offer review](offer-review/)**: Before you sell a packaged service, see it the way a buyer will: what they get and what is left out, what answers each doubt they raise, what protects them, and whether there is an honest reason to decide now. The weakest point is named and one change drafted, from your facts only.
- **[Press pitch preparation](press-pitch-preparation/)**: An owner or marketer with one story to offer gets the outlets and writers it genuinely fits, each backed by a piece they recently published, and a short pitch draft for each. Every claim in a draft comes from facts you supply. Drafts only: a person decides and sends.
- **[Recorded sessions into articles](recorded-sessions-into-articles/)**: A coach, trainer or consultant hands over transcripts of recent sessions and gets back article drafts in their own words: the subjects they actually taught, their key lines quoted word for word, no participant named, and a review sheet of what to check before publishing.
- **[Referral programme design](referral-programme-design/)**: Turn the recommendations your work already earns into a habit: when to ask, who on your own list to ask first and for what, what you give back, the words for each ask and the note an introducer forwards, and how every introduction is recorded. You send each message yourself.
- **[Website findability audit](website-findability-audit/)**: An owner or marketer asking why the website is not found gets a plain report from saved copies of their own pages: what stops each page being reached, read or understood, the fixes in order, and the checks only a live look can settle. No ranking is promised.

## How to use one

Open a folder and start with its README. Every folder stands alone, so copy the one you want and leave the rest. The templates are written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Gemini: paste the instruction block and the output format in as custom instructions, or load the folder in a product that reads `SKILL.md` packages.

In every template a person starts the work, supplies the inputs and decides. The template prepares and checks. Anything that sends, posts, pays or changes a record waits for a person to approve it.

## What "checked" means here

Each folder says exactly what was checked and when: who wrote it, that its sources and rights were reviewed, the date of its editorial review, and whether it has been tried on its invented example in a named AI product. Nothing is described as tested for a client unless it was. Test a template on your own past work before your team relies on it; each one comes with acceptance tests for that.
