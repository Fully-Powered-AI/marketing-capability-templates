# Lead magnet plan

A marketer or owner gets the plan for one free resource built on a question buyers really ask: who it is for, what it promises and delivers, a format the team can make in the hours it has, the outline section by section, and whether it needs a form at all.

*Capability template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/lead-magnet-plan/)*

## The outcome

You stop making free guides nobody asked for. The plan starts from the questions buyers keep raising in sales calls, support emails and the website chat, and picks the one asked most that nothing you have published already answers. It names one reader, the point they are at, and their question in their own words, with where and when each version was heard.

From there it chooses one format you actually make, in the hours you actually have, and writes a working title and a one-sentence promise. It then checks that the outline delivers every part of that promise and that the title claims nothing more. Each section says what it gives the reader and which of your existing material it draws on, so the person who writes it starts from what you already know.

The plan can also say no. When every question is already answered on your site, or the only way to make a magnet work would be to claim something you cannot support, it says so and why. It writes no finished magnet, builds no form and publishes nothing; you decide.

## Who starts the work, and when

The marketing lead, or the owner who does the company's own marketing, starts it when they want a free resource for the website, when a magnet someone has proposed needs sorting, or when sales keeps answering the same question by hand. Whoever hears buyers' questions supplies them. The marketing lead approves the plan, and the finished magnet is approved again before it goes public.

## What they supply

- **Your facts.** A short note: what you sell and to whom, who finds you first and who decides, what you can show, what you must never say, what a reader should do next, where a magnet would live, and whether you have an email list and someone who follows up.
- **The buyer questions.** Call notes, support emails, chat logs, or a question log exported from a spreadsheet.
- **What already exists.** The pieces you have published, and material you hold but have not published, such as an internal checklist or a sample document.
- **Hours and forms.** The time you can give to making it, what you make easily, what you will not make, and how long each format takes you.
- **Any choice already made, and ideas already proposed,** with who proposed each.

## What comes out

One plan, in a fixed order: a verdict and a short summary; the reader, their moment and their question with every place it was heard; the format, the hours it takes and the runner-up; the title, the promise and the honesty check; the outline, with a worked example and a closing that offers your next step once; the limits on what it must never say; where it lives and whether it needs a form; five checks against the usual ways a free resource goes wrong; how you will know it is working, in plain sentences with no targets; what was left out and why; and the decisions left to you.

Good looks like this: each part of the promise has its own section in the outline, and every section is built from material you already have.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your hours, honestly.** The effort check is only as good as the time you say each format takes.
- **Where questions are kept.** A shared log with a date, a source, the buyer's role and the question in their words. Any columns work if you say which is which.
- **Your "never say" list.** Results you cannot show, client names without permission, prices, advice you do not give in public.
- **Whether you need a form.** A form only makes sense once you have an email list and someone who follows up; the plan recommends none until then.
- **Who approves.** The person who approves the plan, and the person who approves the finished magnet before it goes public.
- **Your platform.** It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from the notes and files you paste or attach; it needs no connection to your website or email system.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
