# Lead magnet plan: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The firm, its people, its documents and every buyer question below are invented. The firm is an invented nine-person company that runs IT support for small architecture practices. Its owner does the marketing and wants one free resource for the website.

The inputs come first, in full, so the template can be run on them. The plan follows.

## The inputs

### Your facts

```
What we sell: managed IT support for architecture practices with 5 to
  40 staff: the help desk, file storage and backups, and the computers
  that run design software. A fixed monthly fee per person.
Who finds us first and who decides: the practice manager usually finds
  us; a partner decides.
What we can show: our new-client onboarding checklist (internal, not
  published); a sample handover sheet filled in with invented details,
  used in sales meetings.
What we must never say: that a switch is finished in a set number of
  days; that a practice will be fully secure or compliant; any client's
  name or results without their written permission; prices anywhere
  but the pricing page; comparisons naming another IT provider.
What a reader should do next: book a 30-minute call from the website.
Where a magnet would live: the Resources page on our website. Live.
Email list: none yet. Nobody follows up on sign-ups.
Where leads are recorded: the owner's call notes.
Approves the plan: the owner.
Approves the finished magnet before it goes public: the owner.
```

"I can put about six hours into this over the next month. I write; I

**In short**

Verdict: plan this magnet.

A checklist for practice managers getting ready to switch IT providers, the question buyers asked most (three times) and one nothing on the website answers. Publish it with no form until there is an email list and someone to follow up.
