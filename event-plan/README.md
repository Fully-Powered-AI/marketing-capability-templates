# Event plan

Whoever owns one event, a talk, a dinner, a webinar or a stand, gets one plan: the goal and target, who should be in the room, every step before, on and after the day with a date and an owner, and how each conversation is followed up. Nothing is booked, paid or sent by the assistant.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/event-plan/)*

## The outcome

An event stops being a date in the diary and becomes a short project with a point. The plan opens with the one thing the day is for, such as conversations with a particular kind of buyer, and the numbers you have set for it. It says who should be in the room and what the organiser has actually told you about who comes, with the gaps already turned into a question for the organiser.

Every step has a date counted back from the event day, skips the days you are closed and names the person who does it. Talks get an outline, a run-through and the slides sent on time; dinners get invitations three weeks out; webinars get a registration that lands in people's calendars and reminders that arrive when they matter; stands get two people on every shift who can hold a real conversation.

The day is built around conversations, not headcounts. Everyone working the event takes the same short note after each one, and the follow-up is set before the day: a personal note within two working days for anyone who agreed a next step, one short note for a good conversation without one, and nothing pushed at people you never spoke to. Every invitation, payment and follow-up waits for your approver's go. The assistant registers, books, pays and sends nothing.

## Who starts the work, and when

The person who owns the event starts it: an owner, a founder, a marketing lead or the person giving the talk. Dinners and webinars start about four weeks ahead; talks, stands and visits about three weeks ahead. It runs again whenever the date, the role or the guest list moves, and the new version lists what changed.

The event owner settles every question and approves the plan before it goes to the team.

## What they supply

- **The event brief.** What the event is and who runs it, your role or roles (attending, speaking, hosting a dinner, hosting a webinar, running a stand), the date and place, who approves anything that goes out, and the days you are closed.
- **The goal.** The one outcome the day is for, and any numbers you have set for it.
- **The audience.** Who you want in the room, and what the organiser says about who usually attends.
- **Your facts.** A short note in your own words: what you sell, who buys, the proof you may show and the one next step you offer people.
- **The team, the people list and the organiser's terms.** Who works the event; the groups you will invite or ask to meet, with counts; and the organiser's deadlines, fees and recording rules.
- **Your costs and your record.** Your own figures for tickets, travel and hours, and where conversation notes and follow-ups are kept.

## What comes out

One plan in a fixed order:

1. **The event in one line,** the goal, your target and what counts toward it.
2. **The audience,** what is known about who attends, and a proposed opening question and introduction line built only from your facts.
3. **Each role you play,** and the people you will reach, with counts.
4. **Before, the day and after.** Every line dated and owned; every outward line and payment spelled out and waiting for the approver.
5. **The conversation note and the follow-up by tier,** with who writes each note and by when.
6. **Drafts needed, the cost on your figures, and questions for the event owner.**

A good plan can be read by everyone working the event in two minutes, and nobody is surprised by their name or their date.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your offsets.** The defaults invite dinner guests three weeks out and webinar registrants two weeks out. Set your own.
- **Your follow-up and review windows.** The defaults are two working days for follow-up and two weeks for the review.
- **Your asks.** The people list uses five asks, from an invitation to the dinner to fifteen minutes at the event. Add your own if you have others.
- **What counts.** Decide what goes toward your target and which record it is counted from.
- **Who approves.** Name the person who says go on anything that goes out and on every payment.
- **Your platform.** It is written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, working from the notes and lists you supply. It needs no access to your email, calendar or registration tools.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
