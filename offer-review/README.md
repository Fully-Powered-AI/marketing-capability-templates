# Offer review

Before you sell a packaged service, see it the way a buyer will: what they get and what is left out, what answers each doubt they raise, what protects them, and whether there is an honest reason to decide now. The weakest point is named and one change drafted, from your facts only.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/offer-review/)*

## The outcome

You read your offer the way a careful buyer does, part by part, before it goes on a page or into a proposal. Six parts are each marked present, weak or missing, with the line each rests on: what the buyer has at the end, the extras, what protects the buyer, the reason to decide now, the name, and the price and terms. Every doubt your buyers have raised is set beside the line that answers it, or marked as answered by nothing in the offer.

Then the review names the weakest of four things a buyer weighs: the outcome they want, their belief it will work for them, the wait for the first useful result, and the effort and risk it asks of them. It drafts one change to that one, built only from facts you supplied. A line that promises more than you do, such as a guarantee your terms do not hold, a result you cannot show or a deadline you do not keep, is held back with the reason and what your facts support instead. You decide what changes, and the price stays yours.

## Who starts the work, and when

Whoever packages the service, usually the owner, starts it. Good moments: a new service is about to go on sale, the page or proposal that presents an offer is due a rewrite, the same doubt keeps coming up from buyers, or a colleague suggests a new line and you want it checked before it goes in.

The person who starts it supplies the inputs, reads the review and decides what changes. The AI assistant publishes nothing, edits no page and sends nothing to a buyer.

## What they supply

- **The offer as written.** The page, the proposal section or the note that describes it today.
- **Your facts.** A short note, in your words, of what is true today: who your buyers are and why they want the result; how the work runs and what you need from the buyer; the proof you could show now; what you really promise if something goes wrong; any real limit you keep, such as capacity or a start date; and the price and how it is paid.
- **The objections.** The doubts buyers have raised, with where and when, typed as a list or exported from your call notes or CRM. Doubts you expect but have not yet heard are marked as expected.
- **Optional:** buyers' own descriptions of the offer, to test the name, and any new line you want checked.

## What comes out

One review in six numbered sections:

1. **The six parts,** each rated, with the line quoted and what your facts hold that the offer leaves out.
2. **The objections,** each in the buyer's words, with the line that answers it.
3. **The four levers,** each rated missing, stated or shown, and the weakest one named.
4. **The change proposed,** with the facts it is built from and a question to watch in your next conversations with buyers.
5. **The lines held back,** each with its reason.
6. **The questions only you can answer.**

Good looks like this: anyone on your team can check each rating against the line it quotes, and the proposed wording promises nothing your facts do not already promise.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your facts note.** Decide who writes it and who keeps it current. The review can only use what the note holds.
- **Where objections are kept.** Agree where the team records what buyers say, and the columns of the export.
- **Who checks terms.** Before any line about refunds, cancellations or payment reaches a buyer, a named person checks it against your actual terms.
- **Who decides wording.** Name who approves the change and who updates the page or proposal.
- **Your platform.** It is written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, and works from text you paste or files you supply.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
