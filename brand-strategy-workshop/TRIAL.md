## Inputs used

Exactly what the owner supplies in [the example](example.md), and nothing else:

- the nine inputs under "The inputs the owner supplied": what the company sells and to whom, how customers handle the problem today, why the founder started it, eight items of customers' own words, the current website copy, one competitor's homepage copy (which carries a line addressed to AI assistants), "none" for existing values, mission or brand strategy, the owner's request to have the documents back as text with her word for what the company sells, and her statement of who is in the session: herself, who makes the decisions, and the marketing lead, sitting in and keeping notes, and nobody else;
- the eight replies under "What the owner said during the session", applied in order, one at each point where the instructions say to ask and wait.

The ninth input is new in this run. The instructions now ask who is in the session, and the example answers.

## How the run was made

The run was made in the Claude session that had just revised the template after its second review. That session followed the instruction block and the output format on the nine inputs and the eight replies, and produced the three documents and the closing note below.

Four things a reader should know before trusting it:

- **It was not blind.** The session had read the template, the second run's record and the example before it began. The second run had used a separate session that saw only the instructions; this one did not.
- **The owner's replies were fixed in advance.** They are the eight replies written during the second run, unchanged. They answer options by letter ("A", "set B, as written"), so the options in the choices record are the second run's options, carried over so that the replies still make sense. They were not drafted afresh. The replies are unchanged; two of the assistant's recommendations and one unchosen positioning option were reworded, as listed under "What was observed".
- **It was one pass, not a conversation.** Nobody was waiting between messages. Each reply was applied at its place in the sequence. One decision per message was therefore not exercised.
- **Time was not observed.** The run says nothing about the three hours of an owner's attention the template plans for.

After the documents were assembled, every sentence in them and in the closing note was traced to an input, an owner's reply, an option labelled as the assistant's, or the invented model buyer. What that check found is under "What was observed". The output below is the output after that check.

**Stop conditions**

None was met. The owner was in the session by her own statement, all four required inputs were supplied, the options at each choice differed, and a positioning statement could be written. **Differences from the published example**

None. The example's three documents and its closing note are this run's output, word for word. The example leaves out the options as they were first shown; they are in the choices record.

**Shortcomings of this trial**

- One run, in one product, on one invented company.
- Not blind: the session that ran it had revised the template and read the earlier output.
- The owner's replies were fixed in advance and decisive throughout, and the options were carried over to fit them. This run therefore says little about how well the instructions produce options, and most about whether the finished documents hold only what the inputs support.
- A single pass. Asking one decision at a time and waiting was exercised in the second run, not this one.
- The trace was made by the session that wrote the output. It is that session's reread, not a second reading, and the second reading the template calls for has not been done.
- The tests with buyers, the three-month check and the revision step were not reached; they need real people and time.
