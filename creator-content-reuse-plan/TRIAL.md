# Creator content reuse plan: tried on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-09

The inputs were the invented example in [example.md](example.md): Fernhollow Candle Works, five invented creators and their invented posts. No client data was used. The six inputs are the ones under "The inputs" in that file: the rights record, the published content list, the channel list, Fernhollow's rules, the request and the last reuse plan.

This record is of the last of four runs on 9 November 2026. The earlier three, and what was changed after each, are summarized at the end.

## How it was run

A fresh, non-interactive session was started from the command line with every tool switched off, so it could read no file. Its instructions were the template's instruction block followed by the output format and the line under it, exactly as the template says to paste them, and nothing else from the template. Its one message was the six inputs, copied from the example, under the line "Here are my inputs for this run." The session never saw the example's output, its introductory paragraph, the procedure or the acceptance tests. The account's own general instruction file, which holds business notes and nothing about this template or its example, may also have been loaded, as it is in any session on that account.

## What was observed

**Every required part was produced.** All nine sections and the coverage block, nothing stopped for. No header mismatch, no missing input, no password or payment detail in the inputs.

**Against the published example.** The two plans agree on every status, date, day count, placement, paid option, question, take-down and decision. The differences are wording and grouping:

- Section 1 splits some posts into more lines than the example does (P1's email and deck on separate lines), and adds that the deck's still is not granted for P1 and P2. The example's "Can change it" lines for P1 and P2 also mention that the brand marketer could ask for the email in a trim; the run named the granted cuts but did not add that.
- The run gave the input dates in the Inputs line in long form ("14 November 2026"), lists the code decision for P1 as two lines, one per placement, and in section 4 adds that the piece sits apart from reviews and ratings.
- The example was brought into line with the run on one point before this record was written: it now lists P5's newsletter and deck as not granted as well as held, where it had said "P5 on every channel: held".

**Facts not in the inputs.** None was found in the run's output. "Answer for each" in the P4 channel question and "boost" in the P4 paid question are wording, not facts.

**Where the instructions were ambiguous or needed a guess.** None that changed the result in this run. The run read "Inputs missing: none" while noting the channel list is undated, which is what the template intends.

## The earlier runs, and what changed after each

1. **An earlier run, by Claude Sonnet 5.5, in a session that also had the rest of the template in view** and had seen the example's introductory paragraph. It reproduced the example's statuses, dates and placements and raised five points the template did not settle: whether a date computed from months counts as a rights date; when "no product page for the product shown" applies; whether to offer a granted cut when the channel's usual cut is not granted; whether a paid option ends at the rights end or the planning window's end; and the date of an undated channel list. The template now settles each: a computed date is a rights date; that state is used only when the channel list has no page for the product; no other cut is offered, and section 1 names the cuts the record grants; a paid option's latest end is the rights end; the Inputs line reads "channel list undated" when it has no date. The instruction block also gained the options line, the product-tag rule and the rule that product pages for other products are not listed.
2. **First fresh run, Claude Opus 5.5, set up as above.** Same statuses, dates and placements, with four departures from the template: P3 labelled "not granted" instead of "refused"; ", approved version" added to every approval reference though no post was edited; P5's hold given to the content approver instead of the marketer; and section 9 filled with take-downs, publishing steps and the contract reviewer's questions. The instruction block now names the section 1 states, who clears each hold, when ", approved version" applies, and exactly which decisions section 9 lists.
3. **Second fresh run, same set-up.** Those four were right. Two new departures: the P4 channel wording became four questions, one per channel, and the request's conditional "Instagram ad if any rights allow it" was listed in section 1 for every post without paid rights. The template now says one question covers every channel a single wording leaves unclear, and that a conditional ask is listed only where it waits on the contract reviewer.

## Changes made after this record

After an independent review on 10 November 2026, a few small changes were made and the template was not run again:

- The instruction block gained two lines the procedure already held: a missing disclosure line puts "[open]: no disclosure line supplied" in every credit and disclosure cell and makes the plan partial, and when a stated and a computed rights end differ both are shown. It also now says that one question covers paid use and paid platforms when both are silent, which is what this run did for P4. None of these changes what the example's inputs call for.
- The example's "Can change it" lines for P1 and P2 no longer mention asking for the email in a trim or crop, so they now match this run, as noted under "Against the published example". A closing line now says its placements table is written without the output format's outer pipes, and its paid option quotes the two rights record cells as they appear instead of a rendering of them.

## Shortcomings of this trial

It is one recorded run by one model, on an invented example whose answers the template's own author also wrote, after the template had been adjusted on what earlier runs got wrong on this same example. It shows the template can be followed to the example's result from the instruction block alone; it does not show how it behaves on a real rights record, with messier headers and wording. The acceptance tests in the template were not run, and nothing has been tested in a client environment.
