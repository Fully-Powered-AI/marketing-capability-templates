# Creator content reuse plan

A brand marketer sees which published creator posts may be reused on which of the brand's channels, read only from the usage rights they supply, with every placement ending before the rights do. Where the rights are silent or unclear, the question goes to whoever reviews contracts.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/creator-content-reuse-plan/)*

## The outcome

Your creators' best posts keep working for you, on your product pages, in your email, on your own social accounts and in front of retail buyers, and none of them stays up a day past what your agreements allow. The plan starts with the honest part: which posts you asked for cannot be used, and why. Then it lists every placement the rights support, each with its start date, its end date, the cut, the credit and disclosure that sit beside it, and who publishes it.

Nothing rests on a guess. A channel counts only when your rights record names it in plain words. When the record says nothing, or uses wording that needs reading, such as "the brand's digital channels", the plan quotes it and puts the question to the person who reviews your contracts. The workflow template never interprets an agreement, and that channel stays unused until the answer comes back.

The plan also keeps you tidy. It lists the placements from earlier plans that must come down now or soon, the posts whose rights are about to end and are worth asking about, and the decisions only your owner can make, such as running a post as a paid ad or leaving a creator's discount code on show.

## Who starts the work, and when

The brand marketer or the creator programme lead starts it when someone asks "can we use this creator's video on the product page?", when an email or a launch needs creator content, and once a month to catch rights that are running out. It runs again before each publishing date, because rights end on their own schedule. The marketer reviews the plan, sends the open questions to the contract reviewer, takes the paid and code decisions to the owner, and hands each approved placement to the person who publishes on that channel.

## What they supply

- **Your rights record:** one row per creator, exported from your roster or typed, with the rights end date (or the number of months from posting), the channels granted, whether paid use is granted and on which platforms, the edits allowed, the territory and whether credit is required. The plan reads it; it never fills a blank.
- **Your published content list:** one row per live post, with the product shown, the date posted, the approval it went out under, how the disclosure appears in the post, any discount code shown and whether the post is still up.
- **Your channels:** each channel you run, what kind it is, who publishes on it and the cut it usually takes.
- **Your rules:** the disclosure line you place beside reused creator content, your credit line, which platforms you will pay to advertise on, what a visible creator code does, your cleared product statements, and who plays each role.
- **The request:** the as-of date, the planning window, which posts and channels you want, and any post you want flagged.
- **Your last reuse plan,** if there is one, so placements already live are checked too.

## What comes out

One plan in a fixed order: what was asked for and cannot be done, the rights for each post on the as-of date, the proposed placements with dates and credits, what each cut must keep whole, the questions for the contract reviewer, the paid options for the owner, the rights worth asking about, the take-down list, the decisions waiting, and a coverage list of what was read, what was missing and what could not be judged. It is a draft: nothing is published, boosted, sent or removed.

## What has to change for your company

Keep a rights record in one place, one row per creator, in plain words: dates, channels, paid platforms and edits. The workflow template reads only what that record states, so the clearer it is, the more you can use. Name the person who reviews contracts and answers rights questions, whether that is in-house, an outside adviser or the owner. List your channels with the person who publishes on each. Write the disclosure line and credit line you want beside reused creator content. Decide which platforms you will pay to advertise on, how many days ahead counts as "expiring", and who decides on visible codes. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the files you supply, with no access to your social accounts, your store or your ad accounts.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
