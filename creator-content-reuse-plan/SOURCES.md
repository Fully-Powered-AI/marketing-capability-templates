# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words, from the content reuse workflow template in its own Influencer Marketing set, which it publishes under the MIT licence as the influencer-marketing repository. That workflow template took ideas from five published packages, credited here. From aaron-he-zhu/aaron-marketing-skills (Apache-2.0), its content amplifier: a short, fixed list of rights states judged on a stated date, with anything short of active kept out of new use; a list of placements already live that people take down by hand when rights lapse; no ranking of placements without a rule the person approves; and a list of rights worth extending. From the same package's guide to breaking one piece into smaller units: smaller cuts of one piece that stay tied to the original. Also from aaron-he-zhu/aaron-marketing-skills, the templates of its landing optimizer: the rights have to cover the whole time a piece is on show, not only the day it goes up, and creator testimonials sit apart from customer reviews. From finsilabs/awesome-ecommerce-skills (MIT), its UGC campaign management package: rights recorded channel by channel, only cleared content shown, and a check before publishing that the product in the piece is the product on the page. Also from finsilabs/awesome-ecommerce-skills, its user-generated content package: a verified-buyer label marks real customers, and paid use needs written permission. Also from finsilabs/awesome-ecommerce-skills, its video commerce package: a product tag names the exact product the piece shows. Fully Powered AI added the five rights states with an expiring window; the rule that only words the rights record states plainly count, with silence and unclear wording sent to the contract reviewer as questions; the cuts and what each must keep whole; the brand's disclosure line beside any piece whose disclosure sits only in the caption; the owner's decision on a visible creator code; paid options only where both the rights record and the brand's own rule allow them; and the take-down list read from earlier plans. No third-party text is reused.
