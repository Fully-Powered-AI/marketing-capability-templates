# Ninety-day marketing plan: one trial on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Template version:** 0.1.0

This is a record of one run of the template as it stood on this date. The inputs are the invented example published with this workflow template: Quillmoor Bookkeeping, its customers, the accountants and every figure in them are invented. No client data was used, and nothing was sent, posted, bought or booked. One run on an invented example shows how the instructions behave once; it is not a test in anyone's working environment.

The run was made by a model that did not write the template. It read the template, then only the invented inputs of the example, and produced the plan before reading the example's own output. The comparison with that output was made afterwards.

## The inputs used

The five input blocks of [the worked example](example.md), used unchanged: your facts, money and hours, the results log (ten rows, May to October 2026, exported on Friday 13 November 2026), what you already have, evidence of demand (three items) and ideas being weighed (five items). There was no last plan. The fenced instruction block came first, then the output format, as the template directs.

## What was observed

**Every required part was produced.** All twelve numbered sections and the header lines of the output format are present. The log was totalled twice (15 enquiries, 12 conversations, 8 proposals, 5 won, 1 repeat sale), the by-source figures add to the totals, and the stage came out as First customers, with the owner's view of Repeatable recorded as an open decision. Nothing in the inputs made the instructions stop: the log headers match, every row reads, what is sold and who buys are given, and no input contained an instruction, a password or bank details.

**Differences from the published example's plan.** The two agree on the stage, the log totals and by-source figures, the Won-by-month line, the north star value, the service sheet as the stuck item, the five items already done, the three channels ruled out in the facts and the owner-and-coordinator ownership split. They differ in these places:
- Moves. This run has six, the example seven. The example has a check-in call with each customer as a Keep and expand move; this run leaves Keep and expand without a move and folds the customer ask into Refer (M6).
- The checklist. This run made the tips-and-payroll checklist part of a website move and an experiment (M2). The example lists the checklist under Deliberately not done, because the website has not yet turned an enquiry into a proposal.
- The accountants. This run files the accountant notes under Refer (M5); the example files them under Reach.
- Kill criteria. This run proposes three (the mixer, the website, the customer ask); the example proposes one, for the mixer, with a count of 2 and a read date of Monday 1 February. This run reads the mixer after December alone, on Friday 8 January, which is early, since one mixer's enquiries may arrive after the month ends.
- Scores. Positioning and Evidence of demand are 2 here and 1 in the example. The other seven scores agree.
- Open decisions. This run has six; the example has four. Here: whether each owner can do their lines in the hours given, what happens to the unspent budget, and the newsletter's list were added, and whether a customer may be named was not (the plan simply never names one).
- Dates. This run prepared the plan on Monday 9 November 2026, the date of the run. The example says Friday 13 November 2026, the date the log was exported.
- Newsletter. Both leave it out as a move and say why.

**Facts in the outputs that are not in the inputs.**
- In this run: the kill-criterion counts (0 and 2), the read dates and the date Friday 20 November for decision 1 are the plan's proposals, not input facts. The template requires a count and a date on every experiment, so they cannot come from the inputs; but step 13 also says "every figure traces to an input" (see below). The coordinator "publishes after approval" and "a way to ask for a first conversation" on the new page are this run's own additions.
- In the example's plan: Q3 says the buyers are owners "who already work with an outside accountant", which no input says (the log only shows accountant-sourced enquiries do well); "a referred owner who has a first conversation often reaches a proposal" is an inference from five conversations and four proposals; the date Friday 20 November, the count of 2 and the check-in calls in the third month of each new customer are also its own.

## Where the instructions were ambiguous or had to be guessed

1. **Which "prepared" date.** The example's log was exported on Friday 13 November 2026 and the example says it was prepared that day. This run used the real date of the run, Monday 9 November, so the log export is dated after the plan. The template does not say whether the preparation date is today's date or the log's export date, nor what to do when the export is later. No stage or full-month result changed, because November is not yet a full month either way.
2. **"Every figure traces to an input" against required kill counts.** Step 13 of the procedure and the instruction block demand that every figure traces to an input, yet every experiment needs a count and a read date that the plan itself proposes. The template does not say that proposed counts and dates are exempt, or how to choose a count.
3. **The date a month's row is complete.** Kill criteria are read on a date that depends on the owner having updated the log for the month before. The template says the owner updates it "each month" but gives no day, so a read date could fall before the row exists. This run chose the 8th and the 5th by guess.
4. **Reach or Refer for accountants.** Refer is defined as "customers and others who bring the next buyer"; Reach as "how a buyer first hears of the business". An outside accountant who sends a client fits both. The two plans placed them differently.
5. **Scoring.** The scale mixes how finished a document is (0 to 3) with whether anyone used it (4) and whether it appears in the log (5). The template does not say what to do when a surface has several items (this run took the highest), when an item covers only part of a surface (positioning is stated in the facts and half on the website: scored 2, the example scored 1), or when a surface produces results with no material (referrals: scored 0). Under the scale as written the website scores 5 although it has won nothing.
6. **"Owner: one role, with hours".** The text asks for hours on the owner line; the output format shows only "Owner: [role]". This run put the hours on the first two moves only and omitted them elsewhere, as the example does.
7. **One owner per move, but two people on one line.** The service sheet move needs the owner's decision and sending and the coordinator's finishing. Naming one role as owner meant choosing the coordinator for the sheet and the owner for the sending, which the format does not allow.
8. **Held for experiments.** When the experiments are paid from other lines, the format's "Used by M[n] / Not spent: reason" works, but the template does not say whether the experiment money may be spent on an experiment that is already paid by another line, or what decision to open about it. This run opened decision 4.
9. **The cost of attending the mixer.** The facts show a membership but not what a mixer costs; the plan could not say whether attending needs money without estimating. This run assumed none, and the template gives no rule.
10. **Hours and the bookkeeper.** The bookkeeper delivers the work but has no marketing hours, so owns no move. The template does not say how to handle moves, such as asking customers, that the named bookkeeper would do at the next stage. This run only named them in the next-stage section.
11. **Stage rule wording.** "At least one source produced a new sale in two or more of those three months" was read as per source across Aug, Sep and Oct. The sentence "the Repeatable bar below is met" at the top of the Beyond the founder line refers to a bar that is stated after it. Months with no rows in the log at all are not addressed.
12. **Reported but not logged.** The facts report that four accountants "have referred a client or said they would", which is not a sale. This run treated "Reported but not logged" as sales only; the template says "sales or work your facts report", which could cover it.
13. **What counts as content.** The checklist and the website page could be called content, which "appears only as a move, with its owner and hours" and whose subjects belong to a content plan. This run treated the checklist as part of a website move and chose no subjects beyond the one the owner's idea named.
14. **Where decision dates come from.** The date Friday 20 November for the sheet decision is not in the facts. The template does not say whether the plan may propose a date for an open decision.

Nothing in the run tried to make the plan do any of the things it is told to refuse, so the stop and refusal rules were not exercised. The acceptance tests in the template, which say they have not been run, were not run either.

## Edits made after this run

The template and the published example were revised on 9 November 2026, after this run and after an independent review, to settle what the run found. These edits have not been run again. The output and the observations above are as recorded; where they compare this run with the published example, they describe the example as it stood at the time of the run. Each edit is listed with whether the output recorded above fits it.

To the template (the procedure, the pasted instructions and the output format):

- **The prepared date** (ambiguity 1). It is the date the plan is prepared, never earlier than the log's export date, and the log is supplied with its export date. The run's Monday 9 November, before the export on Friday 13 November, does not fit.
- **The plan's own figures** (ambiguity 2). "Every figure traces to an input" now excepts kill-criterion counts, read dates and decision dates, which are the plan's proposals and are labelled as proposed. The run's counts and read dates fit; its date for decision 1 is not marked "(proposed)" and does not fit.
- **The day the log is updated** (ambiguity 3). Your facts now give the day of the month by which the log is updated for the month before, and every read date falls after it. Without that day, the plan writes "log update day not stated", reads on or after the 8th and opens a decision. The run had no such day in its inputs: its read on Friday 8 January fits, its reads on Friday 5 February do not, and it did not open the decision.
- **Reach or Refer** (ambiguity 4). Asking a known referrer who is not a customer for introductions is Reach; asking customers, and thanking anyone who refers, is Refer. The run's accountant notes under Refer (M5) do not fit.
- **Scoring** (ambiguity 5). Where a surface has several items, the highest counts; an item covering only part of a surface scores at most 1; notes score 1 until gathered into one complete document. The run's 2 for Positioning and for Evidence of demand does not fit; under the revised rule both are 1, as in the example. The website's 5 and the referrals' 0 are unchanged by the rule.
- **Owner hours and two people on a move** (ambiguities 6 and 7). The owner line in the output format now carries that person's hours as given, or "hours not confirmed"; a move that needs two people has one owner and names the others under "Done by". The run's hours on two moves only, and its split owner on M3, do not fit.
- **What counts as an experiment.** It is now a new source or channel, or any move whose reason rests on a belief to test; a move that finishes or fixes what the inputs show is missing, or asks more of a source the log shows producing sales, is not. The run's three kill criteria fit.
- **The stage rule's wording** (ambiguity 11). The Beyond the founder line now points to the bar given in the Repeatable line, and a full month with no rows is read as zero new sales. Nothing in the run depended on either.
- **Decision dates** (ambiguity 14). The plan may propose a date for an open decision, marked "(proposed)".
