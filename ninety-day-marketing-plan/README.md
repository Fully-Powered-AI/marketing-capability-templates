# Ninety-day marketing plan

An owner or first marketing hire gets the next ninety days of marketing on one plan: the stage the business is really in, the moves that fit it, who owns each one, the money and hours they use, and what is deliberately left undone. Every count comes from your own records, and no budget is invented.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/ninety-day-marketing-plan/)*

## The outcome

You stop guessing at marketing and start running it. The plan opens with where the business actually stands, read from your own results by a rule anyone can check: still launching, winning its first customers, selling repeatably, or selling and delivering without the founder. Everything after that fits the stage.

Each move says why it should work for your buyers, what number will show whether it is working, who does it and who owns it. A move whose result is not yet known for your business carries its stop rule, written today and never moved afterwards. The ninety days are laid out fortnight by fortnight, with the stuck items cleared first.

The plan is as honest about what it leaves out as about what it puts in. Tactics you considered and set aside are listed with the reason and what would bring them back. Costs nobody has measured stay marked unknown. The plan spends only the money you gave it, uses only the hours you gave it, and sends nothing: every note, post, call and payment is made by a person after the owner approves.

## Who starts the work, and when

The owner, or the first marketing hire working for the owner, runs it at the start of a quarter, when the business changes stage, or when something it rests on moves: a new offer, a lost channel, a budget that grows or shrinks. A refresh run carries the last plan's stop rules forward unchanged and reports each one as written.

The owner, or whoever holds the budget, approves the plan and settles every open decision in it.

## What they supply

- **Your facts.** A few lines: what you sell and to whom, how you differ, what you can and cannot say, the channels you will not use, the relationships you can draw on by role, who sells and who delivers, and the stage you believe you are in.
- **Money and hours.** Your monthly budget by line, or none; what you hold back for experiments; the hours each person gives to marketing and to selling.
- **Your results log.** A simple spreadsheet, one row per month per source: enquiries, conversations, proposals, new sales and repeat sales. A business with no sales yet starts without one.
- **What you already have.** Your website, sales material, lists and drafts, each with its status and anything blocking it.
- **Evidence of demand, if you hold it.** Dated customer notes, emails, a research summary.
- **Ideas you are weighing,** if any.

## What comes out

One plan in twelve fixed sections: the plan in short; the frame, with three demand questions answered from your evidence; the current state, with nine surfaces scored from your materials; a section each for how buyers hear of you, how a conversation becomes a sale, how customers are kept and expanded, and how they bring others; money and hours; the ninety days by fortnight; what changes at the next stage; what is deliberately not done; and the measures, owners and open decisions.

A good plan can be checked line by line against the inputs, every move appears in a fortnight with its owner, and nobody on the team learns of a commitment for the first time when they read it.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your results log.** Agree the columns, who updates it and when. The template reads seven columns by name; rename yours or tell it which is which.
- **Your stage rule.** The template's bar for selling repeatably is a new sale in each of the last three full months, with one source producing sales in two of them. Set your own if that does not suit your sales cycle.
- **Your surfaces.** Nine are scored by default; swap any that do not apply, such as a website for a business that sells only by referral.
- **Who approves.** The person who approves the plan, and who takes each outward step once it is approved.
- **Your platform.** It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the files and notes you supply; it needs no connection to your systems.

What to write, and when, is a separate piece of work: the [content plan](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/content-plan/). This plan decides only whether content is a move this quarter, who owns it and the hours it gets. ## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
