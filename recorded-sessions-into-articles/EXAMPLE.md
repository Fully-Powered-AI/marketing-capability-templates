### The article draft

**ARTICLE 1: How do you get a meeting to end in a decision?**

Category: Running meetings

Sessions: 6 October 2026, 13 October 2026

The question it answers: how to make a team meeting end in a decision

Two weeks running, the workshop came back to the same trouble. Odalys Venn opened the first session with the one thing she sees go wrong in almost every meeting she sits in on: nobody knows what kind of meeting they are in. Her answer starts before anyone enters the room: "If you cannot say what is being decided, you are not ready to meet."

Four lines carried the teaching, in the order she gave them.

1. "Name the decision before you open the discussion." Write the decision as one sentence before the meeting. If the sentence will not come, the meeting is not ready. She repeated this line a week later, because half the room admitted to skipping it.
2. "One decision, one owner." The owner is the person who will be asked about the decision in a month. That is not always the most senior person present.
3. "Write the decision down while everyone is still in the room." Then read it out loud. Her test: "If two people hear it differently, you have not decided yet."
4. "A decision without a date is a wish." Before the meeting ends, say who does what, and by when.

The mistake the room made was treating the sentence as paperwork. One participant tried it and reported that the team argued about the wording for twenty minutes. Odalys counted that as a success: "That argument was the meeting." The disagreement was always there. Writing the sentence brought it out in twenty minutes, not after weeks of email.

This applies to meetings that exist to decide something. A participant asked what to do when the point is only to share updates. Her answer was to say so and stop calling it a decision meeting: "An update is a broadcast." Send it in writing and give people the time back.

Try it this week. Take one real meeting from your calendar and write its decision as a single sentence. If you cannot, cross the meeting out. If you can, name the owner, write the decision down in the room, and put a date on it.
