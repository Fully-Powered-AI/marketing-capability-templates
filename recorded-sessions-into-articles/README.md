# Recorded sessions into articles

A coach, trainer or consultant hands over transcripts of recent sessions and gets back article drafts in their own words: the subjects they actually taught, their key lines quoted word for word, no participant named, and a review sheet of what to check before publishing.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/recorded-sessions-into-articles/)*

## The outcome

Your best material is spoken once, to one room, and then it is gone. This workflow template keeps it. Every class, workshop or training session you record becomes written work that sounds like you, because it is built from what you said: the subjects that got real time, your own lines in quotation marks, the mistake the room kept making and the exercise you set.

Nothing is put in your mouth. A quotation is your words from the transcript, exactly, or it is not a quotation. The people in the room stay private: no participant is named, quoted or described in a way that points to them. You read a short review sheet, check the flagged details, and publish from your own platform when you are satisfied. The workflow template publishes nothing.

## Who starts the work, and when

The expert starts it, or the person who handles their content: an assistant, a marketing coordinator, a studio manager. Run it after a week or a month of sessions, when the recordings have piled up and nothing has been written, or on a fixed day each week so one article is always waiting for review.

The expert reviews every draft and is the only person who publishes.

## What they supply

- **Transcripts of the sessions.** Any transcript will do: a meeting recorder, a video platform’s captions, a transcription service or a typed record. Each needs its date and title. Notes alone are enough to find the subjects, though not to quote you.
- **A one-page settings sheet.** Your published name and field, how a session recording is recognised, which speaker is you, your tone and the words you never use, your categories, article length, the articles you have already published, your closing invitation, and how many articles you want from each run.
- **The privacy list.** The people who are never named, in every form their name could take, whether a question may be reported as “a participant asked”, and confirmation that participants were told the session was recorded. It can sit on the settings sheet.
- **The dates to cover.** Which sessions count this time, with today’s date and who is running it.

## What comes out

1.  **A review sheet,** read first. How many sessions were read, which articles were drafted and why, every detail the expert must confirm, what was taught but not written, and what the assistant could not judge.
2.  **One article draft per subject.** The title is the question the article answers. The body follows the teaching: the moment that prompted it, the principle, three to six of the expert’s lines word for word with what each asks of the reader, the common mistake, when it applies, something to try, and the closing invitation.
3.  **A check list under each article.** Every quotation with the date of its session, and every point the transcript left unclear.

Good looks like this: the expert can find any quoted line in the transcript in under a minute, and nobody who sat in the room could be picked out from the page.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your settings sheet.** Tone, words you never use, categories, article length and the closing invitation are yours to set once.
- **Your privacy list.** Every participant who speaks on a recording, in every form of their name, and any employer or client they might mention.
- **Your recording practice.** How participants are told a session is recorded and that you may write from what you teach. The workflow template asks whether they were told; it does not decide what your notice should say.
- **Your cleared statements.** The results and qualifications you are willing to state in public. Anything else is left out.
- **Your rhythm.** How many articles per run, who prepares them and on which day you review.
- **Your platform.** It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Decide where transcripts are kept and who may open them, because they hold other people’s speech.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
