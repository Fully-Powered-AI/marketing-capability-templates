# Creator outreach drafting: one run on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Fable 5.2 (model ID claude-fable-5-2)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Template version:** 0.1.0

The inputs for this run are the invented example's own: the outreach settings, the creator list and the request for Halvard Bakeware's Winter Loaf campaign, as printed under "The inputs" in `public/example.md`. The brand, the campaign, the offer, the creators, their posts and the talent agency are all invented; no client data was used. This is a record of what the template produced once, on one set of inputs. It is not a test in any company's environment.

## How the run was made

The assistant that made this run had not written the template and was reviewing it independently. Before producing anything it read `member/template.md` in full and, from `public/example.md`, only the opening paragraphs and "The inputs" (the file's first 57 lines). It did not open the example's output, or any earlier trial record, until its own output was saved to a file. Two things it had read bear on the comparison, and a reader should weigh them:

- The example's opening paragraphs list what the output shows: a payment term held back, a fee in a note not followed, a Partial draft for the creator with no post, three creators with no draft, and a manager version. They say nothing about wording, but they do tell a reader which decisions to expect.
- The template's version notes describe earlier trials and what was edited after them: a follow-up to a manager opens with "Hello again."; the words after a gap in the opener, "is why we are writing", are kept; the post date is printed as given in the creator list; "video" is written for YouTube and TikTok and "post" for Instagram; the closing line is printed once. Where this run agrees with the example on those points, it is because the template now says so.

The assistant followed the fenced instruction block and the output format in `member/template.md`. It counted words with a short script, using the template's definition of a word. One correction was made inside the run, before the output was saved: the first message to the manager counted 121 words without its greeting and 124 with it, over the limit of 120, so its second and third sentences were shortened and it was counted again at 116, or 119 with the greeting. The self-check line for that message says so. The output was then saved, and only after that were the example's output and the earlier record read.

Nothing in the template or the example was changed after this run.

## What was observed

Every required part was produced: the run summary with all its lines, three creator blocks with status, version, the thing they made, why them, the first message, three follow-ups, the self-check and the to-fill line, and the closing line once at the end. Nothing in the inputs made the assistant stop: the header row matched, the standard offer was complete, and no input held a password, a login code, an address or a phone number. It declined only what the template says to decline: the 200 dollar up-front payment in the notes for c-hb03, and the commission fact, which states payment timing and was held back.

The word counts in the self-check were made with the template's own definition: 102, 37, 44 and 31 for Wren Okafor; 119 (greeting counted), 43, 41 and 37 for Tomas Reid; and 37, 44 and 31 for June Adair's follow-ups, her first message not checked because it holds a gap. Every figure in the messages comes from the standard offer, the creator's post, the window or a follow-up fact.

Three things in this run's own output are weaker than they should be, and a person reading the drafts would want to fix them:

- Wren Okafor's opener reads "with the top scored with kitchen scissors", which repeats "with" clumsily.
- Follow-up 2 to Wren Okafor and to June Adair says "If it is not for you this winter". "This winter" is the assistant's gloss on a campaign called Winter Loaf that runs in January; the inputs do not use the phrase.
- Tomas Reid's opener calls the thing he made a "video". The creator list gives its topic and detail but not its kind; the assistant inferred it from the platform (YouTube) and the fit reason ("long-form pie and bread videos"). The example makes the same inference, and calls Wren Okafor's a "post" in the same way.

## Where the instructions were unclear

Nothing was changed after this run. These points are for a person adapting the template.

- **Whether the greeting counts.** The template leaves the subject line and the sign-off out of the word count and does not mention the manager's greeting. This run counted it, and gives both counts.
- **The word band for a manager.** The first message to a manager has to carry the greeting, the opener, the brand, the offer, the ask, the agreement sentence and the rate card request within 120 words. This run's first attempt went over and had to be cut. A person adapting the template may want a wider band for the manager version.
- **"In the settings' words".** The settings give what the brand sells as "We make enamelled cast-iron bakeware, sold on our own online store." This run turned "We make" into "Halvard Bakeware makes", so that the brand is named, and kept the rest. Whether the brand's name belongs in that sentence is not said.
- **What kind of thing the creator made.** The opener is to name "the thing they made", but the creator list has no column for whether it is a post, a video, an episode or an issue. This run avoided the word for Wren Okafor and inferred "video" for Tomas Reid.
- **"Names the creator in its first sentence".** A follow-up to a manager opens with "Hello again." and names the creator "in its first sentence". This run read that as the sentence after "Hello again."

## Shortcomings of this trial

It is one run by one model on one invented input. The assistant read the instructions from the template file, not from a custom-instructions field in a chat product, and it had read the template's version notes and the example's opening paragraphs, which describe earlier trials and what the example shows. It exercised the paths this input holds (a direct message, a manager, a missing post, the skip, do-not-contact and unknown-contact stops, the held-back fact and the fee in a note) and none of the others: a renamed header, a missing standard offer, a possibly under-18 creator, an uncleared product statement, a request to send, or a creator who has replied. It does not show how the template behaves in ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini, or with a real client's list.
