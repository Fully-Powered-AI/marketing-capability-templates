# Creator outreach drafting

A brand marketer gets a first message and three follow-ups for each chosen creator, written for the way that creator can be reached, opening on something they made and stating only the brand's standard offer. A person reads every message and sends it from the brand's own account.

*Capability template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/creator-outreach-drafting/)*

## The outcome

Every creator on your list gets a note written for them alone. It opens on one thing they made, with its date and a detail that shows someone looked, says in a sentence why your brand is writing, and puts your standard offer in plain numbers: what their audience gets, how they are paid, what you would ask for and when. It ends on one small question they can answer in a word.

The follow-ups are already written. The first two each add one fact you supplied, every follow-up is shorter than the first message, and the third says it is the last note about this campaign and leaves the door open. A creator reached through a manager gets a businesslike version that asks for their rate card. A creator who only takes direct messages is never chased by email.

The messages promise nothing you have not offered. A fee, usage rights, exclusivity and payment timing stay with the written agreement and the person who approves deals; a follow-up fact that turns out to be one of those terms is held back and shown to you. Your team keeps every relationship in its own hands: the AI assistant prepares the words, and a person sends each message.

## Who starts the work, and when

The brand marketer or the creator partnerships lead starts it once the creators for a campaign are chosen, ideally from a vetted shortlist, and the standard offer is written down. It runs again for creators added later, or when the offer changes before anything goes out. The person who sends reads every message, fills any gap and sends it from the brand's own account, then stops the follow-ups for anyone who replies.

## What they supply

- **Your outreach settings:** the brand and what it sells, your audience in a line, the campaign, the standard offer in four lines, who approves anything beyond it, which account sends on each channel and how messages are signed, and a few short facts for the follow-ups.
- **Your creator list,** exported from your shortlist or roster: each creator's name, handle, platform, how they can be reached, the shortlist's verdict, why them, and the one thing they made that the message will open on.
- **The request:** which creators, and the date.
- **Optional:** product statements a named person has cleared for outreach, the platforms you do not work on, and how far apart the follow-ups go.

## What comes out

A run summary, then a block for each creator drafted: the status, the version for their channel, the thing they made, the first message and three follow-ups with when to send each, a ten-point self-check, and anything still to fill. The run summary names who got no draft and why, which follow-up facts were held back, any defaults used, and any instruction found in your files that was not followed.

A creator who is excluded, asked not to be contacted, may have an audience under 18, was marked skip on the shortlist or has no known way to be reached gets no draft. A missing fact is marked as a gap for the person who supplies it, never filled with a guess.

## What has to change for your company

Write down your standard offer and name who approves anything beyond it; the capability adds no offer of its own. Decide which account sends on each channel, whether your sender writes as the brand or as themselves, and the sign-off. Choose the follow-up facts you are happy to repeat to every creator, and list the subjects you treat as terms for the written agreement. Match the creator list columns to your own export. Decide how many follow-ups you send and how far apart. A person at your company decides whether your outreach meets the rules on unsolicited messages where your creators live, taking advice where needed. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from files you supply, with no access to your social accounts or your inbox.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
