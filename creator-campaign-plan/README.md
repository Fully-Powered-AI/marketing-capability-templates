# Creator campaign plan

Before a creator campaign starts, the marketing lead gets one plan: the goal, each creator's deal and deliverables, a base and a conservative budget checked against the budget supplied, the dates, the set-up before launch and the decisions. A person approves the spend.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/creator-campaign-plan/)*

## The outcome

A creator campaign stops being a list of names and a hopeful number. The plan shows, creator by creator, what each one costs once fees, gifted product, commission and discounts are counted, how many orders each is expected to bring on your own working figures, and what that means at both ends of your margin band. A conservative case cuts those orders, to half by default, so everyone can see the downside before money is committed.

Fee deals are visible for what they are: money owed whatever happens. Each one shows how many orders it needs to pay for itself, and each waits for the person who approves spend. The timeline is worked back from the day posts go live, so a campaign that cannot be ready in time says so in the first draft, not the week of launch.

Every number says where it came from. Nothing is filled in from an industry average; where your team has no figure, the plan says so and leaves the gap open.

## Who starts the work, and when

The creator programme lead or a marketing coordinator starts it once a campaign has a goal, a live window, a budget and a first list of creators, a few weeks before posts are due. It runs again whenever a creator, a deal or the dates change. The marketing lead reads the plan and settles its decisions; the spend approver, whether that is the marketing lead, the founder or the owner, approves the spend and each fee.

## What they supply

- **The campaign settings.** The goal and why now, the products, the live window, the budget and what it covers, who approves spend, the platforms your business allows creators to post on, your average order value, margin band, code discount and commission rate, and the rules you already have for disclosure, claims and reuse rights.
- **The creator lineup.** Each creator, agreed or proposed, with their platform, deal, fee, gifted product, deliverables and the orders your team expects from them, with where that figure comes from.
- **How long each stage usually takes,** from approving the plan to the last round of draft approvals, and who owns each.
- **Past results,** where you have them, such as an earlier campaign's report.

It works from what you supply. No connection to your store, a creator platform or any social account is needed, and none is asked for.

## What comes out

One plan in a fixed order:

1. **The plan in brief:** what the campaign is for, the expected cost per order in both cases, the budget check and the decisions waiting.
2. **The objective and how results will be measured,** set now so the later report measures the same thing.
3. **The creators and their deliverables,** with anyone on a platform you have not cleared set aside and the reason given.
4. **The budget:** per-order figures, the base and conservative cases creator by creator, the check against your budget, and the flags.
5. **The timeline,** with the lead time and whether the stages fit inside it.
6. **The set-up before the first post** and **the risks,** each with this campaign's response.
7. **The decisions,** each with options, a recommendation and the person who decides, then **coverage:** what was used, what was missing and what the plan could not see.

Good looks like this: the person approving the spend can see in one page what they are committing to, what it costs if orders come in at half the hope, and which choices are theirs.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your approvals.** Who approves spend, and whether fees above an amount need a second person.
- **Your platforms and rules.** Which platforms creators may post on, your disclosure wording, the claims creators may make, and how long you may reuse their content.
- **Your numbers.** Average order value, margin band, code discount, commission rate and basis, any platform or app fee, a target cost per order, and how cautious the conservative case should be.
- **Your stages.** The steps between approving a plan and creators posting, how long each takes and who owns it.
- **Your files.** The column names your creator lineup uses, if they differ from the defaults. When they do not match, the assistant stops and asks.
- **Your platform.** It is written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini and works from files you supply.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
