- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-09

The inputs are the invented example in `public/example.md` (Marrow Lane Coffee Roasters, its facts note, the story, the request and the seven saved pages). No client data was used. The run followed the fenced instruction block and the output format in the capability template, version 0.1.0, and was made once.

**Edited after this run, 9 November 2026.** Following the independent review, the template and the published example were edited after the run recorded here, and the run was not repeated. ## Inputs used

- The request, the story, "Your facts" and saved pages 1 to 7, all from `public/example.md`, section "The inputs". Default settings: five outlets at most, a twelve-month recency window, 150 words for the body, 60 characters for the subject, five working days before a follow-up, and "the marketing coordinator" as the approver.

## What was observed

- **Every part of the format was produced:** story check, reader, spokesperson, destination, numbered facts, facts left out, three outlets with fit, one not drafted, three drafts with word counts and fact numbers, follow-ups, pages read, open questions and the status line. Nothing in the instructions made the run stop.
- **Counts checked.** The three bodies counted 145, 141 and 132 words (greeting to the line before the sign-off), against a limit of 150. The subjects are 47, 47 and 54 characters, against 60. The first version of Draft 1 ran to 166 words and was shortened before it was returned, as the template asks.
- **Where the template left a choice to the assistant:**
  - Whether to keep the June departure (F7) in every draft. The template says a stated limit is kept where leaving it out "would mislead". This run kept it in the contributed-article outline, where readers want the practical lesson, and left it out of the other two, where stating "14 cafés on the scheme" is accurate without it. Another reader could decide differently.
  - Whether a follow-up is drafted whenever any fact went unused, or only for a fact the pitch could not carry. This run drafted one only for F8, the fact dated after today.
  - The follow-ups say two cafés "joined" on 1 December. That is true only if it happens; the template has no step for checking a future fact, so it is left as an open question.
  - The brief says "pages for four outlets" but seven saved pages cover four outlets. The run treated pages as grouped by outlet. This is a quirk of the example, not of the template.
  - Which ask to use for Counter & Crema and Eastvale Business Weekly. The request said to ask for a conversation with Ines unless an outlet asks for something else. This run asked for Ines in both and mentioned Sam as willing to talk. The outlets' pages invite a reader story and a news tip, not a named conversation.
  - Whether F9, the spokesperson's view, may be written as "thinks" without quotation marks. This run did so; the template says only that quotation marks are for exact words.
- **Differences from the published example:**
  - The published example asks Counter & Crema for a conversation with both Sam Okafor and Ines Calder; this run asked for Ines only and noted that Sam is willing.
  - The published Business Weekly ask names Ines and Sam; this run names Ines only.
  - Draft 1 here leaves out the 2017 line and puts the deposit and 2 kg tin in one clause; Draft 2 here adds Ines's view (F9) and omits the published draft's "one of the cafés we supply might make a reader story" opening; Draft 3 here does not mention how long Sam has used the tins beyond "since March" and has the same quotation.
  - The follow-ups here carry only the 1 December fact and do not say "sixteen" or restate counts; the published ones say "sixteen", which is 14 plus 2 from two sources dated differently. (The published example was corrected after this run: its follow-ups no longer say "sixteen"; see "Edited after this run".)
  - Fact F7's label is kept as "customer not to be named" with a note that the fact may be used; the published example uses the label alone.
  - The published example puts the open question about Sam first and the availability point second; the order here is reversed.
- **Facts not in the inputs.** None found in this run's output. In the published example, the follow-ups say "sixteen now take their coffee in tins", which is an addition of 14 and 2 and assumes no café left in between; the inputs do not state sixteen. (Corrected in the published example after this run; see "Edited after this run".) Its Draft 2 opens "one of the cafés we supply might make a reader story", which is an inference, not a fact in the inputs. This run's follow-ups use "joined", which is a prediction treated as past by the date it is sent.
- **Reviewer checklist, applied by the same assistant that produced the drafts (so not independent):** every claim traced to a fact number; the only quotation is F10 in Draft 3, unchanged; no figure, customer or result outside the facts; each opening names a piece by title and date as shown on its saved page; each route is the one the page publishes and the paid address is not used; no exclusive, embargo, deadline or time was offered; each body is within the limit and each draft has one ask; the stated limit was kept in Draft 1; each follow-up adds only F8 and carries the 1 December date.
- **Shortcomings of the template, noted:**
  - The step 8 trace is a promise, not a mechanism. The drafts carry "Uses:" lists, but nothing makes the assistant mark where each fact appears in the body, so a reviewer must check by reading.
  - Where several facts could carry a follow-up the template does not say which to prefer.
  - The reviewer checklist's role names (Approver, Spokesperson) are not explained against steps in the procedure.
  - A trial on one invented example, by the assistant that did not write the template but had seen the answer, shows that the instructions can be followed to a complete output. It does not show the capability works with real outlets, live pages or a real spokesperson.
