# Press pitch preparation

An owner or marketer with one story to offer gets the outlets and writers it genuinely fits, each backed by a piece they recently published, and a short pitch draft for each. Every claim in a draft comes from facts you supply. Drafts only: a person decides and sends.

*Capability template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/press-pitch-preparation/)*

## The outcome

You offer a writer something their readers can use, in their own terms, from your own facts. Before any pitch is drafted, the story is checked: what a reader learns from it, what supports it and who can speak to it. If it is not a story yet, you hear that plainly, with what would make it one, instead of a pitch that wastes a writer's time and your name.

When it is ready, you get a short list of outlets with the reason each one fits: a related piece the writer published recently, readers who match yours, and a route for pitches the outlet itself publishes. Each draft names that piece, tells the story in a couple of sentences, backs it with your facts, keeps any honest limit in the copy and asks for one thing. Nothing is invented: no quotation your spokesperson did not say, no figure without its record, no customer named without permission, no news event, no exclusive. The drafts lead with what the reader learns, and what you sell comes in as support.

## Who starts the work, and when

The owner, a marketer or whoever handles press starts it when there is one story to offer: a change in how the business works, a method worth explaining, a result your records support, or a view on something an outlet has just covered. Run it once per story.

That person supplies the inputs, reads every draft, settles the open questions and sends from their own mailbox. The spokesperson approves any line attributed to them. The AI assistant drafts. It sends nothing, fills in no form and contacts no one.

## What they supply

- **The story.** A few lines in your own words: what happened or what you can show, who it matters to, and why now or why it holds at any time.
- **Your facts.** A short note: the company, the spokesperson and what they can speak to, each fact with where it comes from, any words the spokesperson actually said, what must not be said, and the contact line for the sign-off.
- **The outlets.** Public pages you saved, such as an outlet's guidance for contributors, its contact page and a recent piece with its byline and date, or the outlets' names if your assistant can read public pages itself.
- **The ask.** A conversation, a comment or a contributed article, or let each outlet's guidance decide.
- **Optional:** a page on your own site that explains the story.

## What comes out

One document in a fixed order:

1. **The story check.** Ready, limited by a named gap, or not a story yet.
2. **Your facts, numbered.** Each with what supports it, and the facts left out with the reason.
3. **The outlets.** For each: the writer, the recent piece, three fit checks and the angle. Outlets not drafted for are listed with the reason.
4. **The pitch drafts.** Where to send each, the subject, the body with its word count and the facts it uses, and a follow-up only when your facts hold a fact the pitch did not carry.
5. **Pages read and open questions.** Every page with its date, and anything you need to settle before sending.

Good looks like this: you can point to the line of your facts behind every sentence in a draft, and the writer can see in the first line why it was sent to them.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your facts note.** Decide who writes it, which facts the spokesperson has approved for the press and who keeps it current.
- **Who speaks and who sends.** Name the spokesperson, who approves lines attributed to them, and whose mailbox pitches go from.
- **Your limits.** How many outlets per story, how recent a writer's piece must be, the word limit for a pitch and how long to wait before a follow-up.
- **Words you never use** in public, added to the no-hype rule.
- **Your platform.** It is written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. If your assistant cannot browse, save the outlet pages yourself and supply them.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
