# Press pitch preparation: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Marrow Lane Coffee Roasters, Café Brindle, the town of Eastvale, the four outlets and every person named here are invented; the pages below were written for this example, and nothing here describes a real business, publication or writer, or is evidence of coverage. The marketing coordinator at Marrow Lane has one story and four saved outlet pages, and asks for pitch drafts on 9 November 2026.

## The inputs

**The request**

```
One story: our returnable tins. Pages for four outlets are below, saved
on 6 November. Draft pitches for the ones that fit. Ask for a
conversation with Ines unless an outlet asks for something else. I will
review and send them myself. Use your default settings.
```

**The story** (written by the marketing coordinator)

```
Since March we have delivered wholesale coffee to cafés in steel tins we
collect and refill, instead of single-use bags. Ines thinks most small
roasters who deliver locally could do the same, and that the bag is a
habit more than a need. It matters to other roasters and to café owners.
It is not tied to any news; it is just what we do now.
```

**Your facts** (written by the marketing coordinator and checked by the owner)

```
Company: Marrow Lane Coffee Roasters, a coffee roaster in Eastvale that
supplies cafés.
Spokesperson: Ines Calder, owner and head roaster. She has run Marrow
Lane since 2017 and set up the tin scheme herself.

Facts:
- Since March 2026 we deliver to cafés in 2 kg steel tins that we
  collect on the next delivery and refill, instead of single-use bags.
- 14 cafés are on the scheme (customer list, 31 October 2026).
- 2,860 tins collected and refilled, March to October 2026 (delivery
  log).
- Each tin holds 2 kg, the size of the bag it replaced, so each refill
  is one bag not used (packing records).
- Each café pays an $18 deposit per tin, returned when the tin comes
  back.
- Café Brindle in Eastvale (owner Sam Okafor) joined in March 2026. Sam
  is happy for the café to be named and to talk to a writer.
- One café left the scheme in June because it had no room to store the
  tins. Fine to mention; do not name it.
- Two more cafés join on 1 December 2026 (signed order forms).
- Ines's view: for a roaster that delivers within a day's drive, the
  single-use bag is a habit, not a necessity.
- Ines's words: "We deliver within forty miles, so the tin comes back on
  the next run anyway."
- We think we are the first roaster in Eastvale County to do this.

Do not say: anything about carbon or emissions; we have not measured it.
Destination: https://marrowlane.example/tins
Sign-off: Priya Lund, Marketing coordinator, Marrow Lane Coffee
Roasters, priya@marrowlane.example
```
