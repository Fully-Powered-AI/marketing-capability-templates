# Email sequence drafting

People who asked to hear from you get emails worth opening: a welcome, a nurture sequence or a new customer's onboarding, drafted in your voice, each email with one job and one ask, every claim traced to your own facts, and a clear end. You read, load and send them yourself.

*Workflow template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/email-sequence-drafting/)*

## The outcome

Someone downloads your guide, signs up or becomes a customer, and what follows feels like a person looking after them. The first email brings what they asked for and says what comes next. Each one after it gives them something useful before it asks for anything. The last one makes your next step plainly, once, and the sequence ends there.

Every word about your company comes from a short page of facts you wrote, so the emails promise nothing you cannot keep: no invented results, no customer quoted without permission, no discount you do not run, no countdown. Anything missing, such as a link that is not live yet, is marked for you to fill instead of guessed.

## Who starts the work, and when

The marketer or owner responsible for the emails starts it when people have signed up, downloaded or bought and nothing follows yet, when a new guide or offer needs its emails, or when an old sequence no longer says what is true.

That person reads every line, fills the slots, and loads and sends the emails from the tool the company already uses. The AI assistant drafts. It sends nothing, schedules nothing and touches no list.

It is for people who asked to hear from you. Writing to strangers is [cold outreach sequence drafting](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/cold-outreach-sequence-drafting/), a separate workflow template with stricter rules.

## What they supply

- **Your facts.** A short page: what you sell and to whom, the proof you can stand behind, the offers and terms true today, what you never say, and who signs.
- **What started it.** What people did, and the exact words they saw when they signed up about what they would receive.
- **The goal.** Welcome, nurture or onboarding, and the one next step: a first order, a booked call, a finished setup step.
- **What you have to give.** The guide, help pages or setup steps the emails can offer, each with its link.
- **Your settings.** How many emails and how far apart, other emails these people already receive, and optionally an email or two you really wrote, for your voice.

No list of people and no addresses are needed. The drafts use merge fields.

## What comes out

1. **The frame.** What starts the sequence, who reads it, what they were promised, the goal, and every reason it stops, including a reply and a request to stop.
2. **The emails.** For each: its one job, when it goes and why, the subject and preview, a short body that reads well on a phone, and the one ask.
3. **What each claim rests on.** One line per claim, pointing to your facts or your material.
4. **Read before sending.** The slots to fill, the timing to confirm, the requests left out and why, and any questions for you.

Good looks like this: someone who signed up opens each email knowing why it came, and you could point to the line of your facts behind every sentence.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your facts page.** Write it once and keep it current, offers and end dates included.
- **Your sign-up wording.** The emails stay inside what people were told they would receive, so the words on your forms matter.
- **Your shapes and limits.** How many emails, how far apart, and how many words each.
- **Your sending rules.** Who loads and sends, the merge-field format and fallbacks, and the identification and unsubscribe lines your readers' countries require. You supply those lines; the workflow template leaves the slots.
- **Your platform.** It is written to run in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Decide which files the assistant may read for this work.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
