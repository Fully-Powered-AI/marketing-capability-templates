# Competitor profile from public pages

Your marketing lead gets a dated profile of a named competitor, built only from its public pages: what they say, sell and charge, kept apart from what that suggests and the questions it raises for you. Any figure no page states is marked unavailable, never estimated.

*Capability template · Marketing and content · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/competitor-profile-from-public-pages/)*

## The outcome

You know exactly what a competitor is telling your buyers, and you can prove where every line came from. The profile quotes their headline, their packages, their published prices and the proof they show, each with its page and the date it was read. Your team stops arguing from memory and starts deciding from the page.

The profile is honest in both directions. A competitor’s strengths are recorded as plainly as its gaps. When the pages read are silent on a service, the profile says “no mention found” and names the pages and the date; it never says the competitor does not offer it. A logo wall is recorded as something they show, not as a result their customers got. Because every profile has the same shape and a date, you can lay two side by side, or read the same competitor again next quarter and see what moved.

## Who starts the work, and when

A marketing lead or the owner starts it: before a positioning or pricing decision, when a competitor keeps coming up in sales conversations, when a new name enters your market, or on a set rhythm to refresh a profile you already hold.

The person who starts it names the competitor, supplies the pages or lets the assistant read them, reviews the profile and decides what, if anything, changes in your own marketing. The assistant reads public pages only. It creates no account, starts no trial, fills in no form and contacts no one.

## What they supply

- **The competitor.** Its name and homepage address, or pages you saved yourself, each with its address and the date you saved it.
- **Your facts.** A short note in your own words: what you sell, who buys it, how you charge, what you say sets you apart and the proof you show.
- **The depth.** Quick: the homepage, the services or pricing page and the about page. Deep: those plus case studies and recent posts.
- **The last profile,** if this is a re-read, so the new one can say what changed.

## What comes out

One profile, in a fixed order:

1.  **In short and coverage.** Who they sell to and what they are, then one line saying which pages were read, which were not, and which figures are unavailable.
2.  **What the pages say.** Their positioning, what they sell and how it is packaged, pricing and terms, the proof they show, how they describe their work and where they show up. Every line has its page and date.
3.  **What it suggests.** The reading of those pages, labelled as inferred, with strengths and gaps set beside your facts.
4.  **Questions for you.** Three to five, each naming the line of your facts it bears on. The profile recommends nothing.

Good looks like this: a colleague can check any observed line against the page it cites in under a minute, and no number appears that a page did not state.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your facts note.** Write it once, keep it current and decide who owns it. The comparison is only as good as this note.
- **Which competitors, and how often.** Name the short list and the rhythm for re-reading each.
- **Where profiles are kept, and who may read them.** A profile is an internal working document.
- **Who approves public use.** Before any line reaches a comparison page, an advertisement or a sales deck, a named person checks it against the live page.
- **Your platform.** It is written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. If your assistant cannot browse, save the pages yourself and supply them.
